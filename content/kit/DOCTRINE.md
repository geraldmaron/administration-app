# Content Doctrine — the writers' room

How authored content for The Administration is researched, written, priced, and reviewed, and why. `CONTENT_PLAN.md` says *what* content and *how much*; this file says *how to write it well*. Every content agent and skill loads this file first. Where it conflicts with the generation brief's gate list, the gate wins: the validator is final and this doctrine never loosens it.

Grounded in a content-system audit of 2026-08-22, which read every surface, the engine's metric and effect code, the dataset shape, the shipped corpus, and the research behind the rules below. The audit record is in git history; this file keeps the rules.

Amended 2026-09-02 against a scenario grounding audit (in git history), which read 43 full bodies end to end and measured every incidence figure across all 303 shipped scenarios and 911 replies. Every number quoted below as a corpus rate comes from that measurement. Its finding, in one line: the prose is better than it looks and the seam between the prose and the game is broken, so the amendments land in §2 (weight at severity, alignment), §3 (the mechanism sentence), §5 (a schema gap writers must not paper over), §6.1 (the grounded opening), §6.2 (the binding contract), §9 (dead arcs) and §10 (what the reference set is and is not the bar for).

## 0. The one-paragraph version

The player is the President of the United States, making one to three briefed decisions on a week the desk deals, among 195 other governments that act on their own; the game now advances until something needs the President rather than stopping on a fixed weekly clock. The player's score is five readings (approval, treasury, stability, military readiness, standing) and a slow political alignment; nothing wins, and four things end a term (removal, defeat, term limit, walking away). Content never names a country; it is cast onto whichever nation the live board picks, and its human and financial bands are priced against the player's own nation (the dead land where they are borne; the price lands at home). A plain reply shows its price before the player commits and nothing else; an instrument reply also shows odds, the worst case, a war line and who abroad will notice. What a decision did to people, money and mood is hidden until the Record. Every reply costs something, every flag set is read by something, every critical week is earned by the quiet weeks around it, and the world is seen doing things to itself. Plain words a layman reads once; the register of a sharp newsroom, not a policy paper.

## 1. What the player is being put through

**The fantasy** is competence under constraint: the person in the chair when the phone rings, with formal power, real limits, and no clean answers. Not a god, a general, or a founder.

**The loop.** A week that reaches the desk deals one decision, up to three on a cascade week; the game otherwise keeps advancing on its own until something needs the President or 28 days pass, whichever comes first, so a quiet stretch can run past a single calendar week before the next memo lands (`advanceUntilAttention`, `DEFAULT_HORIZON_DAYS`, `packages/engine/src/advance.ts`). The Desk's own standing conditions, the fourteen statutory clocks and the constitutional calendar all feed one stop today, and so does the scenario director; an intelligence warning, a governor's request and a returned consequence do not yet. A scenario is drawn only once the class's own board pressure clears `STAGING_FLOOR` (0.1, `scenario-director.ts`) — the one dial that sets how many quiet weeks pass between scenarios on a calm board. The memo opens on the stakes and the decision; the cabinet briefs in plain words and disagrees; a foreign government's words arrive only as a relayed document; the player reads the price (and, on an instrument reply, the odds, the single worst outcome, and who abroad will notice); chooses; the rejoinder lands the first cost in fiction; the world then moves (NPCs, operators, wars, disasters, Congress, press, opinion, removal, in that order) until the next stop; the Record later shows what the decision did to people, money and mood.

**What the game refuses**: exact numbers as a puzzle, a morally correct option, didactic tone, real living people, real outlets, runtime generation.

**What the player must always be able to do**: trace a metric swing to a decision in the voice of someone in their administration, and tell two options apart by what each buys and whom it costs. A player who cannot do either has met a content defect.

## 2. The score, in plain words

Four live readings, and one public mood that is the real headline.

**`mandate` no longer exists in the engine — do not write toward it.** It was retired: a fixed weighted composite of approval, stability, standing, readiness and treasury health was a reading of the *state*, not of what the governed think of the president, and calling it the president's standing lied to the player in the one number they steered by (`packages/engine/src/metrics.ts`, doc comment). **`approval` is the sole headline.** The other four metrics did not stop mattering; they became what they always were, *causes* — they reach `approval` only indirectly, through the six opinion judgments below, at the public's own drift speed, never as same-week arithmetic. A writer thinking "this reply's stability cost is worth 0.25 of the headline" is describing a mechanism that no longer runs.

| Reading | Plain meaning | Range | Who reads it | How content moves it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **approval** | the headline: the poll number the public actually holds | 0..1 | removal pressure (as `unpopularity`, 36% of it), the Desk | never directly by a reply; it drifts from the six opinion judgments below, which the other four metrics feed |
| **treasury** | the administration's own budget authority, in USD millions | floored at zero, capped at the carry share of the appropriation | the `services` judgment (health vs a 3.5M reference), instrument gates, budget upkeep | `cost.treasury` only (hand-typed, small). `impact.financial` no longer touches it — see the impact-bands paragraph below |
| **stability** | how calm and functional the country is at home | 0..1 | the `security` and `competence` judgments; a floor breach also reads directly into removal pressure | `cost.stability` only, realized at commit through the administration's fragility (a debit lands heavier on a fragile board) and the public's division (a credit lands softer); the impact model never touches it. A cost does not stay: the reading settles a tenth of the way back each week toward the order the country's institutions hold (`STABILITY_REVERSION`, `orderSettling` in `conditions.ts`), so a one-off debit is mostly gone within a season and only a cost that keeps landing keeps order down. A standing program's weekly order is therefore a level it holds while it runs, about a point from rest (emergency powers two), and no fiscal program carries any |
| **militaryReadiness** | how much of the military could actually be used right now | 0..1 | the `competence` judgment, every military rung's capability gate | `impact.institutional: ["forces-committed"]` (fixed −0.05); hand-authoring it is unsupported in practice |
| **standing** | how much the rest of the world trusts the President's word | 0..1 | the `competence` judgment, NPC trust; a floor breach also reads directly into removal pressure | `cost.standing` only |

**Approval** is not a metric a writer sets. It is a public mood with six judgments (prosperity 0.28, security 0.22, services 0.16, integrity 0.14, competence 0.12, unity 0.08), re-weighted by what is happening (a war the country is a party to makes security count 1.7×, whether it is the country's own war or one it joined beside an ally, a scandal makes integrity 1.6×, and prices or joblessness running past where the term began, by 3 points of inflation or 2 of unemployment (`ECONOMIC_PAIN`), make prosperity count 1.45×; a pump price that has climbed a tenth or more over eight weeks (`pumpShock`) makes prosperity count 1.25× and is the reason the surface names, "gasoline prices are climbing") — these numbers are current, checked against `packages/engine/src/opinion.ts` on 2026-09-29. A war the country is party to no longer costs security a flat amount: it rallies the country through the record every bloc reads (a war it is drawn into adds 2.2 valence units halving over 20 weeks; one this government opened by invading adds the Iraq 2003 fit, 1.86 halving over 7; one it opened by blockade or by force the other side answered adds nothing), and it costs security unease with the front's intensity (0.1 at full), and costs the country's own dead on a log scale (0.08 a tenfold, capped at 0.25) once the war record carries a casualty band (`opinion-war.ts`). Prosperity reads the live economy against the term start, and prices that have already risen keep costing after inflation cools (`PRICE_LEVEL_WEIGHT`, `opinion-causes.ts`), so cooling inflation alone does not buy prosperity back. A recession, an inflation shock and an unemployment shock are labels for what the rates are already doing: none bills approval anything of its own (`condition-impacts.ts`), so what a slump or a price shock costs the public arrives through the growth, price and jobs figures prosperity reads, and a scenario that names one should not also charge for it. Impact bands move mood. The headline is not the weighted judgment itself: the country is five partisan blocs (each party's identifiers and leaners, and the independents who lean neither), every bloc reads the same weighted record, and polarization sets how far apart they start and how little the identifiers move on it (`electorate.ts`). Polarization is a stock at the 2020s anchor (1.0) plus what this administration has added or healed: `divisive: true` pushes it by up to one fully divisive week, which is 0.012 of the `polarize` input (`MAX_TURN_POLARIZE`, `polarization.ts`) and closes 2% of the room to the ceiling (+0.25 above the anchor), and the drift gives half of itself back every 104 weeks (`POLARIZATION_HALF_LIFE_WEEKS`), so a wedge term leaves the country more divided for years, not for a month. Unity reads only what this administration added. A writer never sizes approval: a scenario that should move the public moves a judgment, and the blocs carry it. Force and sanctions also divide the blocs by party (Republicans more willing to see force used than Democrats, Pew 2019), so an act of force gains a little among one side and costs a little among the other on top of what it costs everyone through the judgments.

A story about the administration's honesty opens only from one of these:

- four declared costs: `emergency-powers-invoked`, `intelligence-source-burned`, `conduct-in-question`, or a failed covert commitment;
- a cabinet leak (one in three);
- an inspector general's published report;
- an authored `scandal:<slug>` flag.

`legal-authority-spent` does not open one. A story's weight on integrity is its heat × its gravity by charge (`SCANDAL_GRAVITY`: 1 conduct in question, failed covert operation, authored flag; 0.8 burned source; 0.7 emergency powers or a leak traced to the building; 0.6 IG finding; 0.5 cabinet leak), at 0.25 per story at full weight. The scandal salience multiplier (integrity 1.6×) is unchanged.

To follow a running story, a scenario gates on `scandal:live` (dated, so `agedAtLeast` works). It does not also charge integrity for the story it follows.

**Alignment** is three slow axes (economic left/right, authoritarian/libertarian, nationalist/globalist) that accumulate from `pull` on replies. It is a fifth of removal pressure (distance from what the charter's institutions expect) and feeds NPC trust. As measured on 2026-09-02, the vector is nearly empty: **`pull` appears on 2 of 911 shipped replies**, so removal pressure's alignment term is driven by almost nothing the player chose in a scenario and the campaign leaves behind almost no political shape at all. A position reply that takes a side on how the country should be run declares a `pull`; a genuinely technical week does not. The default is a `pull`, and its absence is the thing that needs an argument.

**Removal pressure** is `unpopularity` (i.e. `1 - approval`) 0.331, alignment 0.184, the last ten turns' decision history 0.129, Congress 0.129, `floors` 0.147 — a direct breach of the stability or standing floor, read independently of approval — and a sixth term, **`faction`, 0.08**: how much of the President's own cabinet answers to a rival bloc, weighted by how long that bloc has stood (`PRESSURE_WEIGHTS`, `packages/engine/src/removal.ts`). The first five are the earlier weights scaled by 0.92 to make room for the sixth, so their proportions are unchanged and the six still sum to 1. What the sixth buys a writer: a cabinet the President has lost is now a route to removal in its own right, and it compounds — the bloc ripens over half a fictional year, and answering it by dismissing its leader leaves a resentful former Secretary still carrying the badge. How often a cabinet breaks is calibrated, so a writer can count on it: a founded sixteen-seat cabinet gives about nine to twelve leaks and two to three departures (resignations and new blocs) a term for a Democrat or Independent President who keeps the room; the Republican's founded cabinet departs less (about 1.3 to 1.4 a term, filed for W4). It gives more for one who keeps overruling it, because each overruled call costs that chair 0.02 of loyalty. Once a chair's loyalty is at or below 0.55 its next break is a resignation, and an ambitious chair at or below 0.6 builds a bloc instead; a chair that already leads a bloc has nothing left to declare, so its next break is a resignation: the rival takes the bloc out of the building. A `faction:<id>` or `cabinet-departure:<how>:<role>` flag is a few-times-a-term event, not a rarity. It crosses three beats at **0.45 / 0.52 / 0.6** (`BEAT_THRESHOLDS`, same file — not the 0.37/0.47/0.55 an earlier draft of this doctrine cited), one beat a turn at most, survivable if pressure drops. **Elections**: the electorate is anchored near half; a record moves the undecided part (a landslide is about 61, a rout about 43); midterms cost seats structurally even for a good record. The contest also reads who turns out (hurting your own cells keeps them home, hurting the other side's brings them out), the direction of the last weeks, a named opponent whose strength is fixed at the draw and learned over the term, and statehouses refusing the program; each moves the count by one to three points at most. **Endings**: removed, defeated, term-limited after two terms, or stopped. The ending and legacy rooms print the record; there is still no win screen; the "win" is a credible story left behind.

**A platform pledge moves the public on its own.** The President runs on up to five planks, and each is a dated pledge (`packages/engine/src/pledges.ts`). When one settles, the engine moves the `integrity` judgment directly (for the term: +0.01 kept, −0.02 broken; for a first-hundred-days pledge: +0.06 and −0.12). The week it settles, that delta is the news. The *record* is a standing term in the honesty judgment's own target — `PROMISE_INTEGRITY` in `opinion-causes.ts`: a broken first-hundred-days promise takes 0.12 off it, a broken term promise 0.05, a kept one credits 0.04 and 0.015, and all of it fades on a straight line over `PROMISE_MEMORY_WEEKS` (52). That is what makes the weekly revision carry a broken promise instead of erasing it: the cost deepens to about a point and a third of approval over the quarter after the break and is gone by the anniversary. None of it is damped: polarization acts inside the blocs, on how far the record moves each of them. It leaves strain on each cohort the plank courts (`PLEDGE_STAKES`, weighted by how much that cohort is paying attention). A reply does not settle a pledge; the act the plank names does. Content can gate on `plank:<id>` (the President ran on it) and on `plank:<id>:kept` or `plank:<id>:broken`, and both are dated, so `agedAtLeast` can make a promise come back as a callback.

**A new administration opens above its record, and the President's own side is a floor under it.** Two figures in `opinion.ts`. The **honeymoon** is `HONEYMOON_BONUS` 0.03, on a straight line to zero by `HONEYMOON_WEEKS` 26 — three points of benefit of the doubt, spent long before the first midterm, and nothing a writer can extend. The **partisan base** is what approval is compressed toward: `POLARIZATION_ANCHOR` 0.45 at rest, falling to `PARTISAN_BASE_FLOOR` 0.18 as the President's own side goes. It goes only when both halves are true — the country believes the administration dishonest (`integrity` under `PARTISAN_BASE_INTEGRITY_FLOOR` 0.4) *and* a story is running about it (live scandal intensity). Neither alone moves it, which is why ordinary bad governing cannot reach the caucus-break line (0.36) or 1974's reading (0.24) and grave conduct in the open can. What that buys a writer: integrity is the one judgment whose collapse changes the shape of the whole model, and a scandal arc that reaches it is playing for the ending.

**Magnitudes a writer should hold** (measured on the shipped corpus, not guessed):

| | routine | serious | critical |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cost.treasury` median (USD M) | −10 | −20 | −30 |
| `cost.stability` median | +0.02 | −0.02 | −0.03 |
| corpus range of stability | −0.08..+0.06 | −0.15..+0.09 | −0.18..+0.08 |
| standing | two-thirds of authored values inside ±0.05; extremes −0.13..+0.10 | | |

Severity widens the tail, not the center. A critical week is not ten times more expensive; it is the week where the worst reply can cost −0.18 stability and the best still costs something.

**Weight at severity.** The tail widening in `cost.stability` is not, on its own, weight. Measured across the shipped corpus on 2026-09-02, a critical week carries the same three replies, the same single condition and the same two-to-three particulars as a routine one, and **36 of 50 criticals (72%) price no human life anywhere on the board**. Severity is being carried by prose intensity and by nothing the engine, the Record or the schema can see, which is exactly why a player stops feeling the difference by the third critical.

A critical week reads like the room going quiet. Concretely, and as a floor rather than a formula:

- At least one reply prices `impact.human` — lives, displacement, or a cohort that carries the loss — or the week says in `context.unknown` who is counting and why the number is not in yet. A critical week where nobody can be hurt is a serious week mislabeled.
- Particulars at four or more, and a transmission when a foreign counterpart is cast (§6.1, §6.2).
- The board is wider or the price is heavier than the batch's own routine median on at least two of: reply count, particulars, human-impact declarations. The test is the *gap* between tiers inside a batch, not an absolute — a routine week that suddenly grows a countdown and a body count is the same defect from the other direction (§7 rule 9).

**This is not a uniformity rule.** Nothing here says every critical looks alike, and nothing here licenses inflating a routine culture or economy week. Realism sets the weight: a hostage week, a levee week and a clemency week are heavy in three different currencies, and the only common requirement is that the weight is *in the record*, priced and particularized, rather than implied by adjectives.

**Impact bands** (the banded half of pricing): `human.lives` none / isolated (~3) / dozens (~40) / hundreds (~400) / thousands (~4,000) / mass (~50,000); `financial.scale` says how the money is authorized (discretionary: the executive alone; supplemental: a vote of Congress; emergency: a vote under an emergency designation; structural: a standing program, priced a year at a time) and the week's severity says how big it is: each pairing is priced from a sourced appropriation of that kind (a routine supplemental is the Flint lead response, about $235 million in 2026 dollars; a serious one Zika, $1.5 billion; a critical one Harvey's first supplemental, $20.6 billion), times horizon one-off ×1, multi-year ×1.8, permanent ×3, then drawn around that from the campaign's seed, skewed toward overrun (DECISIONS §6.16). When the fiction states its own arithmetic, write `financial.basis` (quantity, unit, unit cost in 2026 dollars, weeks, and where the unit cost came from): four hundred drivers paid for six months of waiting is a few million dollars, and the audit warns when a basis lands an order of magnitude from its band. Do not type a round figure into prose: the money is drawn per campaign, so the prose says what it is for and how it is authorized, not a sum. Today both bands are resolved against the **player's own** nation (toll per million of the US population, money at today's prices, so a supplemental in year four is priced at year four's dollars) whatever nation is cast; only the population loss itself lands on the nation that bears it (`borneBy`). So a `structural` band costs the same wherever the scenario is cast, and a small country's catastrophe reads as a small per-million toll at home. Write the bearer into the prose, and do not expect the price to scale with the counterpart. **`financial.scale` lands on the bearing nation's own debt, never on the President's own budget authority.** `resolveImpact` emits a `choiceBorrowing` obligation booked through the national-books debt writer — a disaster supplemental is money Congress votes and the general fund borrows, not money that comes out of what this administration can commit to something new. `cost.treasury` is the other book: it is what the reply itself obligates against the administration's own authority, and it is unaffected by this — a writer's cost vocabulary does not change. Institutional tags: `civil-service-strained`, `emergency-powers-invoked`, `legal-authority-spent`, `intelligence-source-burned` and `forces-committed` carry real mood or readiness effects; `alliance-credit-spent`, `alliance-credit-earned`, `legislative-capital-spent` and `conduct-in-question` price nothing in the impact model, but every institutional tag is now drawn to the player in the pre-commit impact row, under "Costs you later" (or "Buys you later" for `alliance-credit-earned`). Declare them because the president reads them, not because they move a number. `conduct-in-question` is the newest and the odd one out: the other eight are things an administration spent, and this is the one thing an act can cost that it never had — the government's own conduct is now the subject, an inspector general, a referral, a hearing about the act rather than about its result. The band must match the prose: nineteen dead is `dozens`; the audit checks it and the writer does not rely on the audit.

**How a toll is felt** (`IMPACT_MODEL_VERSION` 2). A death toll moves approval as the *square root* of its rate, half-saturating at six deaths per million — about two thousand Americans. That shape matters to a writer because the old one was linear in the rate, and at the population this game is actually played at every band an author reaches for sits in the flat part of that line: mass casualties are 149 per million, an isolated toll is 0.009. The result was that forty dead moved approval less than a strained civil service, and the difference between `isolated` and `dozens` was invisible. Under the curve the same five bands come out at roughly 0.4, 1.2, 3.1, 5.9 and 8.4 approval points before damping, monotone, with no step larger than about three times the one below it. What that buys the writer: **a small toll is now legible, and a large one no longer swamps everything else in the week.** Reach for the band the prose earns rather than inflating it to be felt.

## 3. What content can and cannot move

A `position` reply reaches eight effect kinds: metric, opinion (with polarization) and a population decrement through the impact model, relationship (to the cast counterpart), alignment pull, flags, a narrow conflict move, and a press headline the cast appends. A position may also emit gazetteer `CityEffect` / state / sector via `commitment.gazetteer` when `locus.city` (etc.) is bound. It **cannot** cost a minister their seat, move a vote on the Hill, worsen or relieve a disaster, or reach a third country. A war is entered or opened only by the other two commitment kinds: `join` (beside or against a belligerent) and `instrument` (a rung on the ladder), which together are under ten percent of shipped replies. A reply **can** gate on all of those (charter institutions, cabinet attributes, war state, active conditions, coalition, shared membership, policy stance, operator pressure, geography, revealed preference, removal pressure, turn, flags with age).

Five consequences for writers:

1. **Do not promise in prose what the engine cannot deliver.** "This will cost the Secretary her job" is a lie unless a later scenario is written to read the flag and make it so.
2. **A conversation reply ripples to the counterpart only.** Spread to third parties is real in the game but it happens through instrument actions (the ladder), blocs, and the world feed, not through `position` replies. Write spread into the *fiction* (the rejoinder, the press line, a later reader) and into `instrument` commitments where the week earns one; never claim a numeric third-party effect a position cannot produce.
3. **`cost` and `impact` are two different books, not two names for one price.** `cost` is what the act obligates against the administration's own authority: stability, standing, a small treasury line. `impact` is what the act does to the *bearing nation* — lives, displacement, and a financial band that lands on that nation's own debt (§2's money paragraph; `resolveImpact` raises `economy.debt`, never the President's authority). Before the treasury/budget-authority split these were one book, so any reply naming both was the same decision billed twice by construction, which is the rule this used to state and the corpus-wide count it used to quote. They no longer collapse into each other: a reply can honestly carry a `cost.treasury` line for what it spends right now *and* an `impact.financial` band for the larger appropriation that follows — two pots, not one restated. What still reads as one price typed twice is the narrower pairing where the authored treasury line is already heavy (`STAKES_FLOORS.heavyTreasury` or more) and the financial band is `emergency` or `structural` — the shape a writer reaches for when only one number was meant. `scenario-audit-contract.ts` names this `costAndImpactBothPriced`, surfaced as the warning-severity finding `financial-scale-duplicates-cost`: a warning, not a refusal, because the schema cannot read whether two numbers name the same dollars or two real ones — a reviewer can.
4. **Every priced metric carries its mechanism in the fiction.** Before a metric goes on a reply, the writer completes the sentence *"this reply moves X because ___"* using only what some sentence in this scenario already says — a beat, a pressure, a particular, the transmission, the rejoinder. If the sentence cannot be completed from the text, the metric comes off the reply; it does not get a new adjective to justify it. This is a drafting requirement, not shipped text: the brief's reply scaffold carries the field, the reviewer reads it first, and the finished JSON carries only the price the sentence earned.

   The measured failure is not marginal. **151 of 911 replies (17%) move `cost.militaryReadiness` on a scenario containing no military language anywhere** — an orchestra's pay dispute, a ten-year tax holiday for four factories, a cemetery. The Record then cannot name the cause of the swing, which breaks §6 rule 12 outright, and the player learns that the numbers are decoration. Per §2, hand-authoring `militaryReadiness` is unsupported in practice; `impact.institutional: ["forces-committed"]` is the supported path, and on a week with no forces in it the honest answer is that readiness does not move.

   The same test governs `cost.stability` and `cost.standing`, where no rule can catch a wrong attribution: a stability cost billed to the wrong cause reads worse than no cost at all, because the Record will say it out loud.

5. **Sign convention on `cost.treasury`.** The field is a delta to the treasury: negative is money spent, positive is money kept or recovered. A gain booked there is legitimate (letting a rate lapse really does save the money), but the pre-commit line reads "It costs you" over it, so a positive `cost.treasury` needs a reply text that makes the saving the point of the choice. If the money saved is incidental to what the reply is *about*, leave it off.

Pre-commit the player sees, for a `position` reply, two lines: what it spends (treasury and readiness, exact figures) and what it is assessed to do (stability, standing, approval as bands with the room's confidence and the sentence the assessment rests on). The exact assessed figure is the Record's to reveal, beside the band. For an `instrument` reply the board adds the odds with a verdict word first, the single worst outcome, whether this starts or widens a war, a nuclear warning when it applies, and up to three named third parties who will notice. Hidden until the Record on every reply: the impact bands (who died, who was displaced, what it cost at scale, what it did to mood), except what a reply borrows — debt and yearly interest before the choice, booked as choice borrowing when answered. Two consequences: a position reply's *text* has to carry everything the player needs to weigh it, because the board will not; and the hidden part must be *earned by the text*: a player reading the Record should think "I should have seen that," never "where did that come from."

**Accepted, not built (2026-10-12): contested issues widen this list.** The owner accepted a contested-issues program (`docs/audits/2026-10-12-contested-issues/PLAN.md`; rules in `DECISIONS.md` §13). When it lands, a contested-issue item reaches further than the eight kinds above, but only through declared **levers** the engine prices: an issue step, heat (trigger kinds only), pressure on a named member's vote, primary risk, strain on the cabinet officer who owns the issue, service load, a sector signature, a reaction from other countries by bloc or band, fuel for an operator, condition odds, a scandal origin and a pledge. A writer still never sizes an effect. Each lever obeys rule 4 above, each target has a weekly cap, and an item carries at most two levers beyond its issue. The issue's connection map lists the systemic couplings the engine already runs, so a writer must not charge for them again. **Until the program lands, the "cannot" sentence above stands as written:** no reply moves a Hill vote or a minister today, and a draft that promises one is still a defect. This paragraph is rewritten to the shipped behavior in the same change that builds the levers.

## 4. The countries, to a writer

Content targets **bands**, never a nation id (a `nation` cast is a declared, owner-attributed set-piece). Bands: ten archetypes, power tier, wealth tier, military tier, stability band, alignment band, relationship band. Casts: `tensest`, `closest`, `band{…}`, `warCounterpart`, `belligerent{side}`, `none`, `stricken`, and `outlet` (a fictional masthead across the table; the country slot stays home, and an unnamed outlet may be a paper, a network or a show, so the week has to fit any).

What is real and mechanically read: GDP, population, trade share between two economies, great-circle distance between capitals, quality-adjusted defense spending, warheads, organization kind (defense / economic / political) and which compacts exclude each other, traits (aggression, opportunism, legalism, isolationism, risk tolerance, vengefulness, reliability, transparency), agendas, chokepoints and sea-lane dependence, neighbor adjacency, disaster exposure by region. Military branches with counted units exist for the United States, China and Russia only; every other nation carries tiers and branch strengths, not orders of battle.

What is **not** carried, so a writer must not lean on it: per-nation factions or parties (only the US charter authors parties), a media landscape beyond one press-freedom index, ethnic or religious composition, a fine stance vocabulary (four words on trust toward the President, and at founding 104 of the 195 foreign capitals read "on the merits"). Named places now exist as `City` / `State` / `SectorId` rows. Foreign scenarios bind via cities, nations, sectors, and chokepoints — they must not grow US-state gates.

Therefore: describe scale, not counts ("more men on that border than we can match"); describe capability by what it can do this week, not by inventory; describe a partner's politics as pressures on *a* government, not as named parties; put a place in `locus` as a kind of place (a port, a border crossing, a provincial capital) **and**, for domestic economy/disaster/unrest/election copy that names a plant, factory, strike, industrial zone, the Midwest, or a tax holiday, bind `locus.city` (a dataset CityId) and write `{{city}}` in the subject, descriptor, or opening. Plant, factory, industrial-zone, and tax-holiday copy that binds only a state or sector is an audit **error**. Strike and Midwest copy may still bind city, state, or sector. Never free-text a city name; never write "the industrial zone" as a stand-in metro. Unplaced domestic copy of that kind is an audit **error**, not a warning. The ids are the bind. Energy mix is a closed energy-* sector map, not an unused leftover.

**The structural gap is closed: an `instrument` or `join` reply now carries a `rejoinder`.** The decision (2026-09-02) went to the commit-time beat rather than the staged-resolution seam, on realism: a real room does not go quiet when the president gives the order — somebody acknowledges, names the first friction, puts a dissent on the record — while the *outcome* already had three voices (the resolver's narration, the consequence bank, staged resolution sequences). The silence measured in the corpus (78 of 911 replies, every instrument and join, 18% of scenarios) was at the moment of commitment, so that is the moment the field answers.

**The rule that makes these different from a position's rejoinder: they are written blind to the roll.** An instrument's rejoinder is spoken *between* the order and the resolver's answer, so the line must be true whichever branch resolves. Acknowledgment, execution friction, a dissent going on the record, the first irreversible motion ("the carriers move tonight") — never a result, never the odds restated. `rejoinder-presumes-outcome` warns on the obvious violations; the reviewer owns the rest. A join's rejoinder lands after the narrator states the entry, and answers the fact of being in somebody else's war, not how it will go. The backfill landed in W5 (77 corpus replies across 55 scenarios, 3 in the core pack), reviewed in separate sessions; the field is still optional — the "required" flip is deferred until a reviewed job gives the reference set's two orders and the test fixtures their rejoinders too, on the same path the position rejoinder took. There is still no `remembered` on these kinds on purpose: the resolver narrates what was attempted, and a second authored account of the decision would be the same sentence twice.

**A proposed article-bank line names what it needs and says nothing without it.** The feed article is composed from an item that often carries only a country and a headline, so every bank line in `packages/engine/src/article*.ts` returns nothing when its particular is missing, and a piece with no particulars is one or two plain paragraphs rather than four of mood. A figure prints only with what it measures (a bare count is never "the number attached to this"), extent words are sized to the country (`scopePhrase`), no line claims sourcing the item does not carry (officials, a confirmation, a refusal to comment), a masthead's view of the administration is printed only over the administration's own act, and "why it matters" claims only the American stake the item names. `article-coherence.test.ts` holds the mechanical half and the list of retired lines; `node scripts/dev/sample-articles.mjs --out <dir>` composes the whole feed sample for the read the test cannot do.

Per-outcome *articles* for scenario replies are **not in the schema yet** (0/303); the `press` field is a headline string. Until the schema carries coverage, the rejoinder and the press headline are the whole aftermath a scenario can author.

## 6. The decision: twelve rules of craft

Sourced in the audit's research synthesis; stated here as rules.

1. **Meier's negative test, every set.** If one reply dominates, if a careful player would always pick the same one, or if the choice is a coin flip, the set is not finished. A reasonable adviser must be able to argue for each reply in one sentence.
2. **Separable on a named axis.** Two to four replies, each different on speed, breadth, who pays, or who is told. Two replies at the same position on the axis: cut one.
3. **Inputs shown, outcomes hidden.** Show who is affected, what it costs now, and who is watching. Hide only what happens next.
4. **Reference point before change.** State what was normal until this week before stating what moved; without it the framing is uncontrolled.
5. **One valence on purpose.** Do not frame one reply as a gain and its sibling as a loss to steer the player; frame the set deliberately, and let the rejoinder do the reframing afterward.
6. **A reply is something the President says out loud.** Not a menu label, not a verb phrase; and it names the instrument it commits ("Send the team" is not an order).
7. **The tempting reply must cost something the player did not anticipate.** At least one reply in each set should look safe and prove not to be, and the rejoinder is where that arrives.
8. **No moral verdicts from the room.** Counsel argues interests and odds; the moral is the player's.
9. **Sequencing is an option.** One reply in three may be "do X now, hold Y ready"; the lower rung preserves the higher one as a live threat.
10. **Status quo is a real bracket.** Include the do-nothing or do-little reply and let the room argue it is too little too late; the cost of inaction is in `context.inaction` for a reason.
11. **Vary which reply is right with the board.** Gate on traits, trust, tension, cabinet attributes and flags so that the hawkish, cautious, delegating and delaying answers each win somewhere; a pool where "cautious" is right seven times in ten teaches the player to stop reading. §6.2 gives this rule its floor: as shipped, 89% of every condition in the corpus is `turn` or `flag`, so the board barely varies and this rule is effectively unimplemented.
12. **Teach through the cabinet and the Record, never through the brief.** A new rule is never introduced on a week with urgent human stakes. Counsel forecasts direction and horizon ("we will feel this at the next appropriations fight"), never magnitude. The Record names the cause of every swing.

**Which replies buy a term under the price.** A reply that declares a financial impact, routes a domestic signature, spends readiness, or moves a foreign capital now buys a named term under the price line — the exposure or risk the commit actually carries. A reply that spends only a metric cost and opinion does not: the price line is the whole of what it costs, and nothing is owed beyond it. This is a fact about what the engine draws, not a target to write toward; do not pad a reply with an unearned effect kind to manufacture a term.

## 6.1 The grounded opening

The owner's complaint, in his words, is that a scenario "randomly drops a player off somewhere." Measured, it is literal: **34 of 303 scenarios (11%) open on a lowercase word, and roughly a further one in six opens capitalized with the determiner stripped out** — *"levee went in the night and three river districts are under water to the rooflines."* The first sentence of the player's week is grammatically incomplete in something close to one scenario in four.

**The decapitated opening is banned outright.** A first beat may not begin with a bare noun phrase whose article has been removed, and may not begin lowercase unless it opens on a `{{placeholder}}`. §10 bans stock "The" openers and this is what a mechanical strip pass did to that rule: the fix is to *rewrite the sentence* so it does not need the article, not to delete the article and ship the wreckage. "Backup generators failed at the central hospital during peak surgery" is one keystroke of thought away from the defect and is fine.

**An opening is a scene, not a topic sentence.** Before the first beat ends, the player must be able to answer four questions:

1. **Where they are and who is speaking.** A room, a channel, a person by role — the situation room at four in the morning, the Secretary of Homeland Security on a bad line, a folded newspaper somebody left on the table.
2. **What concretely just happened.** A physical, dated, countable event, not a condition of the world. "The canal is running at a third of what it should" is an event; "water security in the orchard belt is deteriorating" is a topic.
3. **Why it reaches this desk, this week.** What broke downstream, who has already argued about it and lost, what authority only the President holds. §7 rule 1 is the same rule from the other side: write the residue of an argument.
4. **What is being decided.** Not that a scenario is happening.

**The particulars floor is four.** Every one of the ten reference scenarios carries four; the corpus median is two and **85 of 303 (28%) carry none at all** — including a critical hostage week where nothing in the record fixes how many people are held, for how long, or how far away. Zero particulars is a defect at any severity and disqualifying at critical. Fewer than four wants an argument in the review record. Particulars are where the digits live (§10); the prose carries numbers as words, and a scenario that names a place, an actor or a quantity in prose without a particular pinning it has spent its specificity where the Record cannot reach it.

**A foreign counterpart speaks in its own words.** **40 of 118 foreign-cast scenarios (34%) carry no `transmission`**, so the player weighs another government's decision on domestic paraphrase alone. The relay is the corpus's strongest single device where it exists — *"If you send people back undocumented, we seal the checkpoint. We will not absorb your overflow"* does more work than three beats of cabinet summary. A foreign cast without a transmission is a warning at routine and serious, and a defect at critical.

**Bind the place.** 18 of 303 scenarios bind `locus.city`. §4 already makes unplaced domestic plant, factory, industrial-zone and tax-holiday copy an audit error; the wider habit it points at is that a week nobody can locate is a week nobody remembers.

## 6.2 The binding contract: content born bound

A scenario is not finished when it reads well. It is finished when the game can say *why this week, on this board, to this President* — and when the week's decisions leave marks the board can later read back. Measured on 2026-09-02, the corpus reads well and is bound to almost nothing:

| | shipped corpus |
|---|---|
| scenarios with no condition, or only an early `turn` floor | **123 / 303 (41%)** |
| of those, also `cast: none` — free-floating on every axis | 95 / 303 (31%) |
| conditions drawn from `turn` + `flag` only | **193 / 216 (89%)** |
| condition kinds in the schema never used once | 5 / 20 |
| flags set and never read by anything | **846 / 910 distinct (93%)** |
| scenarios that read any flag at all | 95 / 303 (31%) |

**1. Gate on real state, not on the calendar.** The live vocabulary is wider than the corpus uses, and every kind in it is read by `scenario-cast.ts` today: `metric`, `alignment`, `relationship` (trust, alignment, tension, tradeDependence, militaryThreat), `counterpartTrait`, `cabinetAttribute`, `flag` with `agedAtLeast`, `turn`, `removalPressure`, `charterInstitution`, `warState`, `conditionActive`, `coalition`, `sharedMembership`, `policyStance`, `operatorPressure`, `geography`, `revealedPreference`, `stateGrievance`, `cohortStrain`, and **`institutionState`** — which reads a named executive body's `delivery`, `politicization`, `backlog`, `vacantDays` or `openMatters` against the engine's institution catalog, validated at parse time. `institutionState` is the richest gate the game has and it is currently read by nothing. It is also the one that makes a week the player's own fault: every field it reads is a number they can see on the Government screen and moved themselves by appointing a chair, leaving a seat empty, leaning on an agency, or letting a backlog build. `openMatters` reads the oversight ledger the campaign now carries — as a count of *departmental audits* at one body, which is never above one, so a gate on two of them is refused. The fuller oversight vocabulary landed on 2026-09-02 and a writer should reach for it instead: **`oversightMatter`** (origin, stage, `public`, `obstructed`, count, across the whole government), **`shutdown`** (`is`, `weeksAtLeast`), **`appropriation`** (`onPriorYear`, `weeksLeftAtMost`) and **`pendingRule`** (status, count). A cell that says it is gated on the shutdown may now actually be gated on the shutdown. What is still prose only: a matter's direction, the continuing resolution, the full-year CR, the debt limit and the CRA window.

Read the vocabulary from `packages/content/src/scenario.ts` and `scenario-institutions.ts` rather than from memory, and do not substitute a `turn` floor for a reading. `turn` says when the game may deal a week; it never says why this week belongs to this board.

**2. Gate-on-nothing is a defect, with one honest exception.** A scenario with no conditions, or only `{kind: "turn", atLeast <= 5}`, can be dealt to any President in any state of any campaign, and 41% of the corpus is in that position — including a **critical** week bound on four of five axes to nothing. The exception is real and it is not a loophole: some weeks *should* deal anywhere. A routine culture or economy week does not need a four-condition gate (§7 rule 9), and forcing one on it manufactures a false dependency the fiction cannot support. But an unconditioned scenario must **say so and say why**, in the brief and in the review record — "this is a universal week; nothing about the board should change whether it lands." Silence is not a declaration. A batch of ten carries at most two or three such weeks, and every one of them still owes its four particulars, its condition-free-but-argued note, and a price with a mechanism.

**3. Vary the vocabulary across a batch, not only within a scenario.** A batch of ten whose conditions are all `turn` and `flag` has not gated on the board; it has gated on the clock. At least one condition per batch of ten should come from outside those two kinds, drawn from the reading the planner actually has: the planner holds the board, the drafter does not, so **conditions are a required field of the brief, not an option in the JSON**. A drafter filling a skeleton fills the slots the skeleton has. That is precisely how 41% got to zero conditions and how `institutionState` got to zero uses.

**4. Read an existing flag before you set a new one.** The flag economy is 93% orphaned: 910 distinct flags set, 77 read. The doctrine already says a reply may not set a flag unless a reader is named (§9); the standard here is stronger and the cheaper habit — **prefer writing an arc reader for a flag that already exists over minting a new one**. A new flag name is a promise with a cost; an existing one is a callback the player has already earned. When a batch does mint, it writes the reader in the same batch or points at a scenario that will read it, and it checks that every flag it *reads* is set by something the game can actually load. Thirteen flag names are currently read by 24 shipped scenarios and set by nothing anywhere, which makes those 24 permanently undealable — authored, shipped, unreachable. That failure mode is invisible in a single body and fatal to an arc.

**5. Write the axes the engine reads and the corpus ignores.** `pull` on 0 of 911 replies (§2), `relationship` on 5%, `gazetteer` / `domestic` on 0.3%, reply `press` on 9%. These are not decoration; they are how a week reaches beyond its own screen. A position reply that takes a political side declares a `pull`; a reply to a cast counterpart moves the `relationship` it just changed; a domestic act that hits a named place declares who it hit.

**6. `topics` is required on every scenario, and is honest about what it is.** All 303 carry tags and the closed vocabulary is validated, but the only consumers today are the audit and the `--topic` selector in `studio-cli`. It is the room's filing system — how a planner finds coverage debt and how an upgrade or retire wave selects its batch — and it is genuinely load-bearing for that. It is not, today, a tie to game state, and no brief should count it as one. Tag accurately anyway: the wave that comes for a defect class finds the scenarios by topic.

**7. And the sentence that ties it together.** §3 rule 4: every priced metric completes *"this reply moves X because ___"* from text that exists in the scenario. Binding without pricing is trivia; pricing without binding is arithmetic. The week is bound when the board explains why it arrived and the fiction explains why it cost what it cost.

**8. Declare the premise (amended 2026-10-17).** A scenario in a state-dependent class (economy, war, disaster, public health, unrest) either gates on that class's board state or declares why it need not: `"premise": { "kind": "universal", "reason": ... }` for a week that deals on any board, `{ "kind": "introduces", "what": ... }` for the incident that creates the state it describes (a bank run in a boom). The audit refuses an undeclared one as `premise-class-ungated`; the ids that shipped before the rule sit in `scenario-premise-debt.ts`, which may only shrink. The class readings are the pulse bands, `conditionActive`, `metric`, `warState`, `coalition`, `operatorPressure`, `stateGrievance`, `cohortStrain` and `crimeTrend`, per class, in `scenario-audit-premise.ts`.

**9. Bind the body and the frontier (amended 2026-10-17).** `sharedMembership` narrows with `orgKind` and `tradeCompetence`, and the matched body is written `{{sharedOrganization}}`, never "the compact" or "the bloc" (`context-body-unbound` counts the prose that still does). The world gives the United States no shared economic body, so a customs or tariff week gated on one is undealable for it until the dataset extends; write the premise the data supports (a bilateral trade measure, a defense partner) rather than a plausible organization. `sharesBorder` gates a week on a land neighbor, and `locus.frontier: "home"` datelines a border site on the counterpart's frontier with the player's own country; use both for a checkpoint the player's own trucks, citizens or troops stand at. The schema refuses a home frontier without the border condition.

**10. A reply's trade act runs through the trade engine (amended 2026-10-19).** A reply that lays a tariff carries `commitment.tariff` and one that lifts this administration's tariff carries `commitment.tariffLift`, and both call the same engine functions the Trade screen signs (`imposeTariff`, `liftMeasure`), with the law found or read off the measure and cited on the decision screen (DECISIONS §8.12). On a `tradeMeasure` cast a tariff answers the pinned retaliation at the gate's response; on any other nation cast it taxes the counterpart's goods. A lift needs the retaliation cast and a `tariffInForce` gate on the same tariff: the default is the tariff their retaliation answers, and `which: "response"` with `of: "response"` is the tariff this administration laid back in kind, so a week whose words trade away the answer lifts the answer and says the original stays; `theirs: true` ends their answering duty on the same day, which a week may say only when the other government has offered it. A lift may carry an authored stability or standing cost for the protection it drops, never a treasury figure, because customs already books the lost revenue. The engine has no timed pause, so a pause is written as a lift. Sanctions are the economic ladder's third rung, and the second rung lays a fixed ten percent on the goods bought most from the partner, so its words say exactly that. The audit refuses a reply whose words impose, lift, pause or sanction without the route that does it, a rate or goods the route does not lay, and a tariff the words never mention (`unrouted-trade-act`, `trade-act-terms-mismatch`, `unstated-trade-act`). The 2026-10-17 rule that a reply never lifts is retired: the second path it feared is the same function, and the owner asked that a stated act be applied. `{{ourTariffSector}}` names the sector of the U.S. tariff a family's retaliation answers, read from the opener's pinned retaliation.

## 6.3 The pulse, honestly

A pulse gate (`inflationBand`, `unemploymentBand`, `costOfLivingBand`, `crimeTrend`, `cyclePhase`) or a market gate (`commodityBand`) says the week belongs to a country in that condition. What such a week may claim: that prices, jobs, the cost of living or crime are where the band says, in words a household uses; who is paying; what each answer does about it. What it may not claim: the figure itself (the engine owns the number, and it moves; a particular or a line that prints it will be wrong most weeks it is dealt), a forecast of where the number goes, or a cause the gate did not read. Use one pulse gate per scenario: each reachability board carries one band, so a week that needs two readings at once needs a board nobody has built. A market week is about the decision around the market (a reserve release, a waiver, supply for an ally), never a typed price, and always carries a do-little answer with `context.inaction` saying what waiting costs.

## 7. Realism: fifteen rules

1. A situation reaching the President has already been argued about downstream. Write the residue of an argument, not a news alert.
2. Say what is not known, and who reported what through which channel. `context.unknown` is mandatory in spirit as well as schema.
3. The room speaks in contractions and short operational sentences; the `context` block is memo voice, full sentences, named offices, no contractions. Do not mix them.
4. Only a critical week earns a countdown, and only for physical reasons. Routine and serious weeks name *who is waiting* (an ally, a deploying unit, a market open, a vote) without reading a clock aloud.
5. Every instrument has a real clock, written as fact, never as a statute name: force runs on a sixty-day clock the {{assembly}} holds; a national-security tariff takes the better part of a year from investigation to signature; an executive order is instant and the weakest tool against a legislature that already said no; a disaster declaration starts with the state asking, not the President deciding.
6. Never assert that force can be used indefinitely, that an order can create authority the legislature declined to grant, that sanctions bite at once or only the target, that a carrier or a brigade is in theater overnight, or that an ally's government is of one mind.
7. Domestic fiscal friction is friction, not paralysis: delayed contracts, delayed hiring, a fraction of a point of growth.
8. Political timing is inside the decision: proximity to an election changes what is decidable, and the `turn` condition exists to say so.
9. Presidential attention is the scarcest resource. If a routine week is written like a siren, the critical week has nowhere to go.
10. Every consequential decision fires at least two ripple channels with different lags (§8). One ripple in three lands where the player did not aim.
11. Alliance damage recovers on a scale of months, not never. Grudges decay; write the recovery as well as the wound.
12. Some ripples land in the other country's domestic politics; a partner's government can fall because of what the player did.
13. Big crises leave institutions and precedents behind that constrain the player later; a flag named as a deed is how the corpus remembers one.
14. Capability is described by what it can do this week and what it costs in readiness, never by inventory or by a figure that is true of one country and false of the one cast.
15. People are roles, not names. Nobody in the fiction has a surname until the campaign rolls one.
16. The refusal copy a writer may quote never states a threshold. `packages/engine/src/instruments.ts`'s `missingCapability` sentences name the capability in the government's words ("the diplomatic reach to rally other capitals behind it"), never an inequality or a bare decimal ("diplomatic reach ≥ 0.5"). Prose written around a gate follows the same rule: describe what the government lacks, never the number the engine lacks it by.

## 8. Ripples and the world's own weather

**Ripple taxonomy** (use the channel names in rejoinders, consequence beats and later readers so the Record can say which one fired):

| Channel | Mechanism | Typical lag |
|---|---|---|
| alliance credibility | allies re-price the value of a US commitment and hedge | 1 turn visible, 3–8 to recover |
| commodity / energy price | a supply action moves a traded price; importers feel it first | immediate |
| trade diversion and legal retaliation | restricting A redirects flows and invites a rules-based challenge by a coalition | 4–8 turns |
| third-country domestic politics | the decision becomes someone else's election or no-confidence issue | 1–4 turns |
| bloc cohesion | a bloc splits between the exposed and the insulated | 1–3 turns |
| institution / precedent | a durable new body or rule that binds the player later | 3–10 turns |
| legal / authorization clock | a timer a rival branch controls starts | fixed |
| domestic execution friction | fiscal or bureaucratic drag slows every other instrument | 1–2 turns, compounding |
| escalation reservation | the lower rung preserves the higher rung as a live threat | same turn |
| reputational contagion | countries with no stake read the act as a forecast about themselves | 2–6 turns |

**NPC ↔ NPC as the norm.** The engine already runs 195 utility agents that act against each other, join wars, fracture blocs and sign compacts; what the player sees of it is intel-gated and under-surfaced (a concealed covert move produces no feed line; a war the player is not in has no drill-in). Content cannot fix the engine's visibility, but it can write the norm into every artifact: pressures in `context` that come from two other governments' quarrel, transmissions that mention a third capital, consequence cards whose feed line is about what a neighbor did in reply, readers that open on a week the world's weather changed while the player was not looking. The real menu of a week, weighted: about half verbal cooperation (calls, readouts, statements), a sixth material cooperation (agreements, aid, arms), a quarter verbal conflict (demands, criticism, protest), under a tenth posture and coercion, a few percent violence. Most of it is not actionable; every third or fourth item should quietly load a variable the player meets later.

## 8.1 The staged act: step-sequence craft

A consequence card is the aftermath, told once the dust settles. A **resolution sequence** is the ordered steps the room watches while it settles: the order signed at ten at night, the crossing, the company that does not come back, the report that lands before the after-action note does. It is pre-authored template data keyed on instrument × rung band × valence × named failure branch, selected deterministically at commit time, because the game makes no model call at play time. `news-editor` owns the prose; the `resolution-sequence-generate` skill carries the loop.

**The honesty rule, in plain words.** The outcome is computed *before the first step plays*. By the time the player reads "the order goes out," the packet is built, the metrics are decided and the branch is drawn. So a sequence narrates a result that already exists. It may not ask whether the operation will work, hint that it might yet go either way, or end on a note the packet then contradicts. A success ends on a gain, a partial ends on a mixed step, a failure ends on a cost, and the schema refuses anything else rather than trusting an author to remember. Manufactured jeopardy is the one defect this family can die of: a player who reads a sequence twice and notices that the suspense was decorative stops believing the first read of everything else.

That is not a rule against tension. Tension in a sequence comes from **sequence**, not from doubt. A failure is worse when the first two steps go exactly to plan. A success costs more when the step before the gain is a loss the player has to look at. What is banned is the false question, not the bad news.

Five things follow from it:

1. **Every step's own turn is settled too.** `held` is the part of the act with no local result yet — an order read out, a column on the road, a port call alongside. It is not suspense. A `loss` costs; a `rescue` gains or is mixed and must have something lost earlier in the same sequence to get back — the schema checks that the rescue and the loss name the same thing, so write the thing into both lines; a `stand-down` is where the act ends and cannot be written mid-sequence.
2. **The last step is the only one that carries the sequence's overall result.** Individual steps before it settle their own local turn — a `loss` costs, a `contact` can gain — and the honesty rule binds the sequence's ending, not every marker along the way. What is forbidden is a step that claims the whole act is decided before the act has finished playing.
3. **Named particulars arrive through slots or not at all** — `{{actor}}`, `{{target}}`, `{{unit}}`, `{{person}}`. A sequence is drawn for whichever pair of nations the act involved, so it can never be a set-piece. Unit designations are generated per campaign, and a step whose verbs need a particular branch or a corps-scale formation says so through `unitAffinity` rather than hoping the draw agrees. No real formation, ship, special-operations unit, outlet or living person appears anywhere. Half of that is gated and half is discipline: `REAL_DESIGNATIONS` in `resolution-beat.ts` refuses ordinal-plus-type formations, four ship prefixes and four named special-operations units, and **nothing in the schema checks for a real outlet, statute or living person** — write "a trade weekly," "state television," "a wire desk" because the rule says so, not because a gate will catch you.
4. **A step carries no effect and must not claim one.** It cannot move a nation stat, seat or unseat a Secretary, or touch the removal arc. Anything that bites landed through the packet before the sequence started.
5. **Restraint holds at the top of the ladder.** The camera stays on the crews, the ramp, the manifest, the health ministry's published list and the officer who learns a village's name from a reporter. Report what was counted, never what it looked like. No moral verdict: the world reacts out of interest, and the player supplies the judgment.

**Reachability is measured, not assumed.** Every authored sequence must answer a key a resolution can actually emit — `uncoveredSituationKeys` reads the matrix one way and `unreachableSequences` the other, and `scripts/verify-resolution-beat-reachability.mjs` gates it. A cell with no sequence falls back to the unstaged path honestly; a cell padded with a sequence nobody wrote is worse than an empty one.

## 9. Replay: what the pool must measure up to

The corpus target is 2,000 done excellently (decided 2026-08-08). Volume is necessary and not sufficient; the pool is held to five measurable properties:

- **R1 Recognizable-surface recurrence.** Over a simulated campaign, no repeated recognizable surface (same subject, same distinctive reply line) within one campaign; at most two across five consecutive campaigns. The director damps repeat *classes* (6-turn cooldown, 14-turn taper) but not repeat *ids*; until it does, `once` and conditions are the writer's only guard.
- **R2 Recontextualization ratio ≥ 3.** Most replay variety comes from casting and conditions (a different nation, chair, board state), not from a new base scenario. Write conditions aggressively; the same week against a different counterpart is a different week only if the text lets it be.
- **R3 Intensity pacing.** Roughly 40% routine / 40% serious / 20% critical inside every class; the director spaces criticals but can only stage the quiet weeks the pool contains.
- **R4 Option-type outcome entropy.** No option archetype (hawkish / cautious / delegate / delay) has the same directional outcome more than about six times in ten across the pool.
- **R5 Reachability and spread.** Every scenario reaches at least one of the preview boards (the gate checks; five shipped scenarios still fail it), and the cast mix moves toward the plan: ~35% band / 25% tensest / 10% closest / 30% none. The shipped corpus sits at 6.6% band and 61% none; a planner weights new cells toward band casts until the mix recovers.

**Before any of this is measured**, the pool has to parse. The pre-rename key `cost.readiness` once failed the strict schema, so the player app dropped the affected scenarios, the ledger commands failed, and `studio scenarios job` could not build a brief. The shard migration to the current key has run, and `scripts/verify-shipped-content-parses.mjs` now strict-parses every shipped rendering of the corpus, so a schema rename cannot orphan scenarios unnoticed. Every corpus number in this file was measured over the bodies as authored, not over what the game could load at the time.

**The flag economy is the largest structural debt**: 910 distinct flags set, 77 read, **846 (93%) set and never read by anything**, and only 95 of 303 scenarios reading a flag at all. Rule: a reply may not set a flag unless the batch, or the arc ledger (`studio corpus arcs`), names its reader; when a batch sets a flag it writes the delayed reader for it or points at an existing one. §6.2 rule 4 states the preference that follows — read an existing flag rather than mint a new one. Closing a promise *raises* the `arc-branch-unread` count because the audit can now see the arc; that is progress, not damage.

**An arc ends, and says how.** A family's close marks every reply `held` or `lost`, at least one of each: held when the story ends on terms this administration set or can live with, even at a price; lost when it ends against it. Judge by what the reply's own rejoinder, remembered line and press say happened, not by which answer is better; the Record prints both at one weight. An arc nobody closes lapses after sixteen quiet weeks, and a close left on the desk ends it the same way: that is a real ending ("it stopped being news and nobody here ended it"), not a consolation. Every opener ships with a close somewhere; an arc with no way out is an audit error.

**The reverse failure is worse, and it is live.** A flag a scenario *reads* but that no reply anywhere sets makes that scenario permanently undealable — it is authored, reviewed, shipped, and can never appear. Eleven flag names are currently in that state, and 24 arc readers are waiting on them: `funded-the-fleet-over-the-clinics`, `hunted-the-leak`, `let-the-strike-run`, `published-the-memo`, `sent-the-team`, `split-the-budget-line`, `stalled-on-the-memo`, `capped-the-withdrawals`, `escorted-the-rescue`, `funded-the-clinics-over-the-fleet`, `ordered-them-back-to-work`. Before a batch ships an arc reader, check that its flag is set by something in a pack the game can load. A dead gate reads as a defect in nothing and costs an entire authored week.

## 10. Register: plain words a layman reads once

The reference texture is a season of a quality political drama and a sharp newsroom: ledes that commit, a particular in every paragraph that has one (a name of a role, a number as words, a refusal), no knowingness standing in for information. The shipped corpus runs about a tenth thinner than its own bar (median near 553 words against the reference set's 605, counting subject, context, opening, transmission, particulars and replies) and its briefings lean on fewer particulars: median two against the reference set's uniform four, with only 17% of bodies containing any digit at all against 30% of the reference set. Write to the reference set (`content/reference/*.jsonl`), not to the floors.

**What the reference set is the bar for, and what it is not.** It is the bar for **particulars, prose and impact**: every one of the ten carries four particulars, every position reply declares an `impact`, and those are exactly the axes the corpus falls short on. It is **not** the bar for binding, and a writer sent there to learn how to bind will learn the wrong lesson. Measured: 0 of its 30 replies carry `pull`, 0 carry a `relationship` move, four of the ten have no conditions at all, and three offer an instrument reply that returns nothing. The original ten model the gap the owner complained about; the bound exemplars now exist — `content/reference/reference-11-14.jsonl` carries `pull` on political replies, a `relationship` moved on a foreign cast, gates on `institutionState` and `oversightMatter` and `appropriation`, and a `locus.city` bound with gazetteer reach — and three of them are what the job brief quotes. **§6.2 remains the authority on binding and the reference set on texture**; the four bound exemplars are where the two meet.

**Register defects the audit found surviving to ship**, because they are the kind a player notices once and never trusts again: a broken substitution (*"Our patrol boat yard our patrol boats can't make next month's payroll"*), an adjacent duplicated word (*"or the front stops meaning anything anything"*), and a lowercase word after a sentence-terminating period. Read the finished body aloud from the first character; the defects are all in the first line or the last. Note the distinction that matters here: deliberate anaphora — *"bought quiet and bought it again a generation later"* — is craft the corpus does well and often, and is not the same thing as an exact adjacent repeat.

Always: expand every term of art in the sentence that uses it; one idea per paragraph; active voice; the first beat states what is at stake for the country and what is being decided; officials have already done the summarizing. Never: "the line" for a budget and similar building shorthand, stock openers (more than half the corpus opens on "The"), a first beat with its determiner stripped instead of its sentence rewritten (§6.1), "nobody in the room can," "in about six weeks," filler, a subject that only says a scenario is happening. American spelling throughout, and US units (miles, degrees Fahrenheit, pounds) where a unit appears in prose; the treasury's USD-millions is a storage unit and never appears in prose. Figures belong in `particulars` values (the reference set carries digits there); prose carries numbers as words.

**American vocabulary, not a preference.** The playable seat is the US presidency and only the US presidency; there is no non-playable charter left to hedge for. On the player's own government, write the real American titles: "the Secretary of Defense," "the Secretary of State," "the Attorney General," "the Department of the Treasury," "the Pentagon," "the State Department," "Congress," "the House," "the Senate," "impeachment," "censure." "The chair" and "the minister" for the player's own cabinet are defects, not house style — the `parliamentary-vocabulary` audit rule (error) rejects them. The rule does not yet catch the hard-coded literal **"the assembly"** for the player's own legislature, which survives in 12 scenarios including one subject line; the templated `{{assembly}}` placeholder is correct and unaffected, and an assembly *area* on a border or an assembly *line* in a factory is a different word. Where the sentence means the player's own legislature, write Congress, the House or the Senate. A foreign counterpart keeps its own government's real vocabulary: "their foreign minister," "their parliament," "the coalition government" are correct, because that is someone else's country, not the player's. The distinction is whose government the sentence is describing, never a stylistic choice.

| Parliamentary / British | American, on the player's own government |
|---|---|
| the minister / the chair | the Secretary (name the department) |
| the foreign ministry | the State Department |
| the finance ministry | the Treasury |
| the defense ministry | the Pentagon / the Department of Defense |
| a cabinet reshuffle | a new appointment, a Senate confirmation fight |
| a no-confidence motion / a vote of no confidence | impeachment or censure — the mechanism that actually exists |
| backbenchers | rank-and-file members of Congress |

A counterpart's own titles ("their foreign minister," "their finance ministry") are never in this table's left column when they describe someone else's government; the table governs only what the player's own administration is called.

The brief's gate list (thirty-three rules with live constants) and its three doctrines (quote the player back, announce the fuse, one dilemma eight shapes, bind the person, answer the player) are the contract; this file does not restate them. Read the brief in full before writing, every time.

## 10.1 Time and tense

Some copy is on screen the moment the player commits, and at that moment nothing has happened yet: the rejoinder, the remembered line (which is also the Record's line), the press headline, the inaction text (which becomes the narration and a wire item dated the week of silence), every line of a consequence card, and every step of a resolution sequence. The next Continue is at most a week away. A line that says "eleven weeks later a researcher published" is contradicted by the first thing the player does after reading it. It also hands every later scenario a fact it has to honor: a rejoinder's "the review took nine weeks and named nobody" was once overturned by the very reader written to answer it.

1. **Commit-time copy stays inside the week the player is in.** Say what is true tonight, by morning, or this week: the order goes out, the call is returned, the first cost shows up.
2. **A forecast is fine when it is one.** A speaker may say "it could take months" or "if the host holds firm, it may be spring before anyone moves." Hedge it, and give it to a person rather than the narrator.
3. **A precedent is fine when it is about an earlier case.** "The last time this happened, it took years" names time that ran out before this decision.
4. **Anything that happens months on is a later scenario.** Gate a reader on the flag the reply set, age it with `agedAtLeast`, and let the months actually pass. That is where "the review came back after nine weeks" becomes true.
5. **An age is read off a deed that happened.** `agedAtLeast` dates a flag from the marker it left when it was set, so it only works with `is: true`. To wait N weeks for something that did not happen, age the opener's own `scenario:<id>` marker and put the negated flag beside it. The schema refuses `agedAtLeast` on `is: false`.
6. **A callback does not state its own age.** A reader arrives at least N weeks later and often much later, so "five weeks ago" in its particulars is wrong most of the times it is dealt. Until a `{{sinceDeed}}` token exists, name what happened, not how long ago.

**What the gate checks.** `scenario-audit-time.ts` reads only the commit-time fields; the opening, the standing brief, the precedent and the reply text may talk about time freely. `consequence-audit.ts` runs the same reading over every line of a card.

| Tier | Examples | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Anchored, past a week | "eleven weeks later," "a decade later," "months later," "the following year," "next quarter," "over the coming months," "by spring," "in autumn," "within the month," "inside eight weeks," "the rest of the year," "by the end of the year," "weeks passed," "months went by," "it was autumn before," "by Christmas" | error (`commit-time-flash-forward`) |
| Anchored, told as a report | "after six months," at the head of a sentence; a past verb in the same clause as "after" or "in" and a stated count: "the vote carried after five weeks," "they found it in eleven weeks," "in eight weeks the convictions were in" | error (`commit-time-flash-forward`), unless the sentence ends its interval at the decision ("had doubled in six weeks") |
| Anchored, inside a week | "two days later," "inside a week," "the following week" | warning (`commit-time-elapsed`) |
| Soft | "took eleven weeks," "spent months," "was off for eleven days," "held through the winter," "eventually" | warning (`commit-time-elapsed`) |

A sentence with a modal or a conditional ("could," "will," "may," "if," "is due," "expects to") is a forecast and passes. A present tense is a schedule or a term and passes: "the first units arrive in two weeks," "the figures are published in six weeks," "the order lapses after ninety days," "retires after nineteen years of service." A comma, "and" or "but" starts a new clause, so "signed, and the first units arrive in two weeks" reads the arrival, not the signing. A sentence that names an earlier case ("last time," "the one before," "historically," a year, "ago") is a precedent and passes. A perfect tense ("has kept that registry for nineteen years") ends at or before the decision and passes the soft tier. "The next morning" and "the following day" are still the night of the decision. Each sentence is read on its own, so a forecast in one does not excuse the next.

**The debt is counted, not waived.** Shipped content carried commit-time flash-forwards before the gate existed. `packages/content/time-framing-baseline.json` lists each error-tier line that ships today across the baked corpus, the consequence cards, the resolution sequences, the pilot pack and the reference set: 142 at recording (123, 7, 6, 4 and 2). The list grew once, to 161 (140, 7, 6, 4 and 4), when the gate learned the report shapes above: each of the nineteen lines it added was read and is a flash-forward. The first rewrite batches brought it to 75, every one of them in the baked corpus: the consequence cards, the resolution sequences, the pilot pack and the reference set carry none. `scenario-audit-time-baseline.test.ts` fails on any line not on the list, and on any listed line that has since been rewritten until it is deleted, so the count can only go down. The rewrite is its own batch, reviewed in a separate session like any other. The reference set goes first, because the brief quotes it to every writer.

## Scenario accounting and subject continuity — implementation contract

An uncertain program cost is an estimate, not a known outturn. The card says “about”; the engine draws the booked obligation only when a reply is committed, independently of previewing, using the existing bounded cost distribution. The Record retains the estimate, the actual borrowing booked at the national books’ precision, the variance, the pricing source and the planning-calibration label. Fixed contractual obligations and ordinary instrument costs retain their existing contract. Old frozen cases without estimate metadata remain fixed; never manufacture an estimate the President was not shown.

`financial.basis` counts the primary component plus optional `components` (label, quantity, unit, unitCostUSD, weeks, source). Its required audit `coverage` explains the included federal components, excluded federal/state/private costs and why the accounting boundary fits the choice. A placement grant alone is not the total federal cost of admitting refugees. Every numeric input needs its own source or an explicit planning label. Do not double-count components or multiply an already-total price by duration again. A band without case-specific arithmetic is a broad proxy, and the interface must say so. The corpus rewrite supplies sourced arithmetic; the engine cannot infer it from the prose. Variation remains a labeled planning assumption rather than a fitted probability claim.

Recurring organizations use the existing `ENTITY_REGISTRY` and `entities`. Optional `subjects` binds named slots: `entity` references that registry; `governor` pins the actual person in the bound state when introduced; `institution` references the existing institution catalog; `inherit` names the predecessor scenario and slot. Use `{{slotKey}}` in text, never an invented person or another copy of the same organization. A frozen case retains the binding; closed cases keep it for later callbacks. Family and flag callbacks inherit recorded subjects and causal origins. A missing predecessor excludes the candidate, rather than fabricating its history. A remembered former governor is a person, not evidence that they still hold office.

Narrative subjects acquire identity and provenance only. A named yard has no new production capability, a clinic has no invented capacity, and a town has no invented population. If existing data cannot support a crop belt, village, industrial specialization or physical feature, do not assert it. Supply a sourced dataset extension or revise the premise in the later corpus pass. A plausible state draw is not proof that every geographic sentence is true. Existing unsupported premises remain on the migration worklist until reviewed.

Small locality context is now available through `locus.settlement` (`small`, `commercial-port`, `road-border`) and optional `settlementId`, sourced from the existing Natural Earth input and specific capability evidence. “Small” is a model population tier, not proof of farms, frost exposure or a particular industry. Unsupported requirements fail closed. Do not give a national unlocated condition a fictional local address. Existing economic cities may bind a named fictional mayor with `subjects.source: mayor`; other local figures remain unnamed offices. Member slots declare House/Senate and state/nation scope. A governor/member/mayor letter or readout declares `transmission.domesticSubject`; officials do not become unrestricted direct speakers. Use `inherit` when the story means the original person, even after they leave office. Case-specific sourced financial arithmetic, supported premise repair/retirement and prose migration remain the next corpus pass.
