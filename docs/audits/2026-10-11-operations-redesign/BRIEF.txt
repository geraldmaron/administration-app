7.1 Typography

Use Source Sans 3 for application UI and analytical data. Retain Source Serif 4 for formal document titles and formal reading text where its longer measure remains readable. These Adobe families were designed to complement each other and have OFL licensing [R2, R3]. This is the selected pairing, not a claim of federal endorsement.

Use @fontsource/source-sans-3 and the existing @fontsource/source-serif-4 package through the current dependency and lockfile workflow; verify the actual available versions and weight import paths instead of copying an unverified latest version. Fontsource provides both families for self-hosting [R30, R31]. Load only used scripts/weights. Start with Sans 400/600/700 and Serif 400/600/700 only where needed. Preserve license notices. Remove superseded Geist imports once consumers are migrated. Do not load a new remote font service.

Use tabular numerals in Sans for data. Monospace is reserved for necessary reference IDs/debugging and can use the system stack; it is not the general government style.

Implement a single role scale in rem, with these starting CSS-pixel equivalents at a 16 px root:

| Formal document reading | 18 / 28 | Issued instruments and sustained formal prose, about 65ch maximum measure. |

Use sentence case. Restrict uppercase and tracking to brief document kickers or small institutional labels. Do not combine tiny type, muted color, uppercase, and wide tracking. Keep ordinary reading measures around 55–75 characters. Do not shrink table text to squeeze in extra columns.

A disabled action remains understandable: show the blocking requirement and how it can change. Do not hide every unavailable diplomatic or legislative act. Keep priority ordering stable unless a material change warrants movement, so the queue does not jump under the pointer.

Do not use one pill style for all five concepts.

Tooltips explain brief secondary information; they are available on focus and never contain the only actionable terms. Rich contextual inspection uses a persistent popover, drawer, or linked dossier, with an explicit close path and manageable nesting. Avoid hover-only chains that fail on touch.

- Domestic policy: policy portfolio, budget, economy, emergency response.
- World and security: world/states geography, country relations, national security, operations.
- Communications: press preparation, coverage, running stories.
- Record: acts, obligations/history, legacy.
- Utilities: administrations/saves, reference, settings.

Use group headings and labeled destinations instead of forcing all routes into a long flat list. Press/communications, emergency response, and operations must have an obvious entry path. A contextual alert can lead directly to a specific crisis, but it is not the only navigation path. Names should match what the player is trying to do and remain consistent between navigation, page titles, documents, and help.

On phones use a compact route header and drawer for the full hierarchy. If the existing product uses bottom navigation successfully, retain only 4–5 stable top destinations with labels and an obvious “More”; do not force every desktop group into tiny targets. Preserve the same information architecture and all primary tasks.

Support entity-first entry (open country/person/office, then choose an action) and action-first entry (choose a presidential instrument, then select its target) through the same underlying action model. Do not fork different pricing/validation behavior for the two paths.

9. DECISIONS, INLINE CONTROL, AND TIME

9.1 A coherent action lifecycle

Use the existing action/pricing/validation system as the source of truth. Normalize the visible lifecycle:

Inspect the situation → compare options → configure supported terms → review the selected act → authorize once → receive acknowledgment → follow modeled implementation → inspect observed results and record.

These are interaction responsibilities, not a demand to add eight clicks or fabricate eight engine states. Combine steps for simple actions. Separate them when the user needs a stable review of consequential terms.

Before a consequential authorization, show:
- Exact action and target, recipient or responsible institution.
- Scope and quantity with units; current baseline and requested change.
- Authority and important constraints represented by the game.
- Upfront and recurring costs when actually modeled, available capacity, and remaining reserve.
- Effective timing, implementation delay, deadline, expiry/review conditions when supported.
- Main tradeoffs, leading affected parties, and a path to the complete relevant set.
- Material uncertainty and the cost of waiting, declining, or allowing the default.
- Whether the action is reversible, replaceable, or immediately committed according to the real model.

Inline interaction rules:
- Filters, sorting, map layers, expansion, candidate comparison, and draft changes are immediate and reversible; no confirmation modal.
- A small low-impact modeled adjustment can commit inline with a clear resulting state and undo only if a true reverse operation exists.
- A consequential action uses an explicit selected summary/review region. A drawer is suitable when context and comparison must remain visible.
- An irreversible or unusually high-impact action gets a dedicated review stage with specific authorization wording. Do not invent a universal modal for every click.
- Text/numeric inputs have labels, units, supported range, meaningful step size, validation, and a reset-to-current action.
- A continuous slider is allowed only when the model supports continuous or sufficiently granular values. Pair it with a numeric input. If the model offers four named stances, use four named choices.
- Use radio choices for a few discrete policy options, searchable selection for many candidates/targets, steppers for discrete quantities, and tables for comparative allocations.
- Do not hide meaningful configuration inside a tooltip or require opening unrelated routes merely to adjust an allowed parameter.

9.2 Known facts, assessments, and forecasts

Classify values before formatting them:
- Authorized input: the exact amount/setting the player is committing.
- Known current fact: what the President can know now.
- Observed historical result: what happened and when.
- Staff forecast: an estimate with a horizon and a defensible basis.
- Confidence: quality/strength of evidence behind the assessment.
- Unknown, unavailable, withheld, or stale: separate explicit states.

Likelihood and confidence are different concepts [R10]. Do not expose exact hidden vote rolls, future outcomes, or secret preferences to satisfy a prettier forecast. Do not add arbitrary probability ranges when the engine provides no basis. A deterministic projection may be presented as a current-model projection with its assumptions; it is not a guarantee of later conditions.

Use recorded exact vote totals for completed votes. Use assessed commitments or clearly labeled projections before a vote, according to the game's knowledge model. Show actual-versus-forecast comparisons in the record when both are available. Distinguish the policy setting, its implementation status, and measured consequences.

9.3 Commit, persistence, and time behavior

Retain or improve duplicate-submit guards, stale-preview detection, validation against changed resources, and save-failure handling. Do not apply a stale action because the visual draft still looks valid. Preserve unsent drafts by administration/campaign, subject, and action type; they must never leak between saved administrations. If time or another action changes available resources or terms while review is open, retain the draft, explain the changed facts, and refresh its assessment before commitment. Specify draft retention on navigation, reload, withdrawal, and switching campaigns.

After authorization, keep the user in context by default and display a durable receipt with the committed terms. “View record” is an explicit secondary action. Remain in context only while it is still valid: honor modeled mandatory transitions, including a concluded conversation, expired matter, campaign ending, recovery, or changed administration. After a successful act, clear or retire its draft and remove obsolete authorization controls. Show the committed receipt or navigate to the required next state; browser Back must not reactivate an already issued draft. If the act applied but saving failed, say that accurately and offer the real recovery/retry path without issuing the act twice. Do not present a failed save as a failed act or a successful save as completed implementation.

Animate acknowledgment only to communicate a real transition. State updates must not wait for a fake transmission, signature performance, processing percentage, or dramatic timer. Keep ordinary transitions roughly 120–250 ms, with motion reduced or removed according to user preference. A long ceremony belongs only to an actual opening/ending event and must be skippable.

Audit time controls together with decisions:
- Ready matter followed by Play, fast-forward, repeated Resume, and route changes.
- A new urgent matter arriving during time advance.
- Last-call deadline and lapse/default consequences.
- Explicit defer, decline, allow default, and authorized delegation.
- Stale preview while time advances.
- Background tab, reload, save recovery, and term transition.

Show why time paused and what action will resume it. Reading a matter is not authorization. A deliberate defer/default choice should clearly say what can happen next. Preserve the existing simulation's intended time model; change behavior only where the audit proves it contradicts the visible contract, and update tests/documentation together.

10. OFFICIAL DOSSIERS AND PRESIDENTIAL INSTRUMENTS

10.1 Dossiers

A dossier is a structured institutional record, not a generic card with a large title and unrelated scores.

Default dossier order:
1. Issuer/record family, as-of date, and honest reference identifier.
2. Subject identity, role/jurisdiction, current state, and a restrained portrait or semantic emblem when available.
3. “Why this requires attention” or a concise current assessment.
4. The few facts needed for the immediate task.
5. Role-specific capabilities, positions, constraints, relationships, or commitments.
6. Relevant actions with their eligibility and terms.
7. Source/evidence, history, related entities, and other details.

Use a consistent identity block; portraits can start around 72×90 px on desktop and reduce sensibly on phone. Do not invent photographic evidence or let large portraits crowd out status. Dense personnel comparisons use aligned rows and a detail view.

Adapt the body to the subject:
- Person: role, portfolio, eligibility, assessed positions, relevant relationship/record, current nomination or assignment.
- Office/department: mandate, incumbent/acting/vacancy, responsibilities, workload, nomination/oversight state.
- Country/state/district: place and jurisdiction, leadership, conditions, interests, commitments, relevant policy/trade/security.
- Judge/court case: judicial office or procedural record, actual decisions and assessed jurisprudence; preserve institutional independence.
- Incident/story: geography/source, allegations versus verified facts, current stage, people affected, response options, history.

Do not invent “classified,” “eyes only,” clearance levels, seals, legal citations, or objective psychological scores merely to make a dossier look official. If a confidence rating, reference number, or source field is not modeled, either derive it transparently from valid existing data or omit it.

10.2 Orders and other instruments

Use document families appropriate to the game's actual acts: executive order, presidential memorandum, proclamation, budget proposal, legislative action, diplomatic instrument, military authorization, or administrative direction where supported [R9]. Do not relabel every instrument an executive order.

A modeled executive instrument should be readable as:
- Issuing office and document type.
- Simulated reference/number when one exists; otherwise a transparent game record ID.
- Subject/title, issuance date, and state.
- Purpose and authority represented in the model.
- Numbered operative sections expressing actual scope, directives, authorized resources, responsible executor, and timing.
- Effective date/trigger, review/expiration, and related constraints where supported.
- Signature/issuance state.
- Linked implementation, legal challenge, superseding act, and historical record where those exist.

Separate the draft workbench from the issued document. Configuration controls edit a draft; an official preview exposes its operative content; the authorization control commits it. The signed/issued document must reflect the committed terms rather than silently changing when a new draft is edited. Do not invent legal authority or fabricated U.S. Code citations. Real archival forms are presentation references, not authorization to change game powers.

Show only lifecycle stages actually modeled. UI draft/review states can be added without pretending they are simulation states. If a missing simulation distinction is essential, document the narrow proposed model change and its compatibility requirements before implementing it; do not invent fields merely to complete a stage rail. Signing, issuance, implementation, in-effect status, a stay, expiration, and invalidation are not synonyms. A seal/checkmark cannot imply a deployment arrived or a policy succeeded.

On a wide screen, use readable paper in the main column and a coordinated terms/status/action rail. On a narrow screen, put identity and critical terms before the document body, with an anchored review/authorization control that does not obscure content. Provide print/export only if already supported or clearly justified by the workflow; do not create a new publishing project.

10.3 Institutional language

Keep labels precise and plain:
- “Authorization,” not “Sign it.”
- “Effective dates and review,” not “Its clocks.”
- “Responsible department,” not “Who carries it out.”
- “Legislative outreach,” not “How to move them.”
- “Collection priorities,” not “What it is collecting.”
- “Federal executive” or the correct institution, not an improvised administrative category.
- “Estimated fiscal effect” or the actual assessment, not “the numbers the resolver rolls against.”

Use correct U.S. titles for modeled offices, and distinguish federal, state, congressional, judicial, and foreign roles. Do not invent exact legal procedures the simulation does not implement. Keep adviser speech human and allow news sources their editorial voice. The institutional interface, quoted speech, journalism, intelligence analysis, and developer/debug text must remain visibly different genres.

11. DATA SIZE, DENSITY, AND VISUALIZATION

Inventory actual current datasets and saves before choosing a collection component. For each class record: source/model, available fields, observed minimum/typical/large count, expected growth, filter/sort needs, update rate, confidentiality/knowledge rules, and comparison task. Do not assume older prose about the number of countries or campaign length is still correct.

Cover people, offices, departments, legislators, nominations, court cases, states/districts, countries, commodities, sectors, bilateral relations, forces, operations, policy proposals, orders, bills, events, stories, obligations, and accumulated records.

Presentation defaults:
- Use rich option cards for a small set of materially different choices. Roughly 3–12 is a diagnostic range, not a rigid universal threshold.
- Use rows/tables for repeated comparable entities, especially when sorting and scanning matter.
- Use master/detail for a substantial collection where opening individual subjects is common.
- Use scoped tabs when the subject has genuinely different tasks; do not hide critical terms in a generic “More.”
- Use a digest/priority queue for heterogeneous incoming matters.
- Use a structured reading surface for formal prose.
- Never use a bare top-N truncation to make the layout pass. Show total/filtered count and provide access to the omitted items.
- Preserve the selected target and draft through filters, sort changes, paging, and detail navigation.
- Show around 5–7 useful comparison columns by default when feasible; expose additional fields through a meaningful detail or column view. Column count is subordinate to the task.
- Align numerical values and units; use tabular numerals and shared comparison scales.
- Distinguish percent change from percentage-point change, stocks from flows, appropriated from spent funds, annual from monthly amounts, and number of assets from number of formations.
- Use zero, unknown, unavailable, not applicable, and stale deliberately; never display an unexplained dash for all five.

Stress with valid representative saves and isolated development fixtures. Include zero data, one row, many simultaneous urgent matters, long labels, and a long presidential record. If the model plausibly grows to 1,000 or 10,000 records, test those scales. Do not invent 10,000 countries or corrupt a real save to manufacture a benchmark.

Do not add virtualization merely because a list has 50 states. Profile real rendering and interaction costs. Prefer pagination or incremental loading for long histories when it preserves reading and navigation. If virtualization is necessary, verify keyboard traversal, focus retention, row semantics, selected items, and full-record retrieval.

Avoid decorative gauges for arbitrary scores, unnecessary donuts, radial charts for unrelated units, unreadable network hairballs, and motion that disguises missing information. Add a visualization because it improves a task, not because every card needs one.

14. VERIFICATION AND ACCEPTANCE

14.1 Visual completeness

- Every current route is accounted for, including transitions and conditional paths. The 48 historical entries are reconciled, not blindly treated as the current count.
- Each route has current before/after evidence at a primary desktop width and a narrow width. Shared layout families receive the full breakpoint/state matrix; unique maps, tables, dialogs, and sticky controls receive their own edge cases.
- Inspect the whole scrollable page. Above-fold screenshots alone are insufficient.
- Typography, spacing, banners, controls, tabs, state labels, icon geometry, and document treatment agree across screens.
- The native icon gallery and actual asset workflows prove semantic coverage and readability. Jets, fuel, crude oil, and other real domain objects have correct identities wherever the player compares or authorizes them.
- No accidental overflow, obscured primary action, duplicated title hierarchy, unreadable low-contrast metadata, clipped long names, or hidden collection tail remains.
- The official feel comes through in language, structure, and provenance as well as colors and seals.

14.2 Interaction and simulation truth

Use meaningful end-to-end checks where the risk warrants them:
- Selecting/comparing a draft option does not accidentally commit it.
- The issued record preserves the exact authorized quantities, units, costs, and target from the valid final review. Current available/deployed/operational/returned/lost quantities may legitimately change; reconcile those dated changes rather than forcing inventories to match the original order.
- Double activation causes one act.
- A stale preview is revalidated or refused with an explanation.
- Applied act plus failed save is distinguishable from rejected act; retry cannot duplicate execution.
- Filters/paging/detail navigation, browser Back/Forward, and closing inspectors preserve selection, scroll, focus, and drafts as designed; campaign changes isolate each administration's drafts.
- Pending-action withdrawal, refusal, retry, and no-longer-available targets have distinct behavior where supported; none invites duplicate execution.
- All omitted collection items are retrievable.
- Forecasts and observed results are distinguishable; hidden outcomes are not exposed.
- Time-control/deadline/default paths obey the documented contract, including reload and background behavior.
- Disabled actions explain actual blockers.
- Document statuses represent true modeled transitions, not cosmetic success animations.
- A consequential workflow can be completed with keyboard and through the non-map alternative.

14.3 Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the implemented UI and document what was actually checked [R11]:
- Normal text contrast at least 4.5:1; qualifying large text at least 3:1 [R12].
- Meaningful non-text controls and graphical boundaries meet the applicable 3:1 requirement [R24].
- Text enlargement and narrow reflow preserve content and functions [R13, R14].
- Focus is visible and not fully hidden by sticky headers, action bars, or overlays; project preference is to keep the whole focused control visible [R25].
- Use semantic headings, forms, tables, names, labels, validation, and status announcements [R26].
- Dialogs manage focus and return it appropriately; tabs follow the correct interaction pattern [R16, R17].
- Avoid color-only meaning and hover-only essential content.
- Provide keyboard operation and a click/tap alternative to dragging and map gestures where applicable. Keyboard support alone does not satisfy the single-pointer alternative requirement [R27].
- Respect reduced motion; don't force unnecessary animated transitions [R28].
- Verify touch targets and spacing under the project contract, distinguishing it from the standard's exact minimum [R15].

Automated checks complement manual interaction and screenshot review. A clean accessibility scan or a snapshot does not certify the entire experience.

14.4 Performance and data-size proof

Record the current baseline and final behavior on the same representative fixtures/device/runtime. Check route changes, large-list filtering/sorting, map selection/layer changes, draft recalculation, and document opening. Identify long tasks, excessive rendering, and growing DOM costs where observed. Preserve responsiveness without decorative loading theater.

Do not claim universal performance gains from a faster development run. Do not add heavyweight charting/map libraries when existing primitives can meet the requirement. Choose a library only for a concrete missing capability, verify its current API/license, and measure its cost. If dependencies or rendering architecture change, check the production build and supported desktop host.

14.5 Required gates and honest closeout

Run the repository's required build/typecheck/lint/tests/catalogue/registry/visual gates in the correct order. Use focused tests for concrete behavioral risks and the required final gates; do not generate trivial tests that mirror JSX or only count routes/words.

If a current copy-budget test conflicts with the improved hierarchy, assess the actual content. Do not conceal critical terms in disclosures, delete necessary explanations, or loosen every budget merely to pass. Update the intentional budget with the design decision and rendered evidence where justified.

A task is complete only when its implementation, rendered result, affected workflow, and required checks agree. Do not claim tests ran when they were only written. Do not mark blocked or unverified work as done. Do not create fake screenshots, silently skip routes, or report a percentage of coverage without the inventory denominator.

15. FINAL REPORT AND CONTINUATION DISCIPLINE

Continue until the authorized redesign and current-route audit are complete, or until a genuine access/capability blocker prevents a specific remaining portion. Keep the active plan and evidence current so the work can survive a context reset without restarting or losing decisions.

The final report should contain:
1. Current checkout/commit context and exactly what was redesigned.
2. The major findings resolved and why the resulting interaction is better.
3. Font, icon, component, document, density, and map/breakpoint decisions actually implemented.
4. Current route/workflow coverage, before/after evidence, and representative completed presidential tasks.
5. Build/test/accessibility/performance checks actually run with results.
6. Authoritative plans/catalogues/tasks updated.
7. Any real remaining defects or blocked verification, precisely scoped.

Do not end with an offer to implement the plan. Execute it. Do not describe a partial visual refresh as a complete audit. Do not reopen superseded dotted-map or historical design requirements.
