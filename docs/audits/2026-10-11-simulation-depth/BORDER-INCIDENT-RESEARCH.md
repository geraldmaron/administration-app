# Frontier incident distinction

Two primary records ground the distinction between an exchange of fire, what officials initially know, and later attempts to establish cause:

- [OSCE Minsk Group statement, 13 July 2020](https://www.osce.org/minsk-group/457225): both sides accused the other of starting a border exchange. The co-chairs described reported fire and called for direct communication to prevent escalation without adopting either attribution.
- [NATO Secretary General statement, 4 October 2010](https://www.nato.int/en/news-and-events/events/transcripts/2010/10/04/nato-secretary-generals-statement-on-his-meeting-with-the-foreign-affairs-minister-of-pakistan): after a fatal border incident, the statement described the act as unintended, said a joint investigation was underway, and called for improved military coordination. It does not establish a general frequency or magnitude for such events.

The engine's border-incident draw and numerical relationship effects are provisional game assumptions, not rates or estimates taken from those records. The records support delayed attribution, an incident-specific inquiry, and a distinction between preventing escalation and rewarding trust. The existing `border-clash` producer remains a government's deliberate hostile-act decision, so its causal stamp must not be relabeled as accidental.
