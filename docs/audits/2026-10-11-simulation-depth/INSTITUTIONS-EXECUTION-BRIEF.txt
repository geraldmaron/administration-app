## 3. Keep legal instruments and institutional powers distinct

Model legislation, enacted law, executive orders, agency rules, court proceedings, judgments, appropriations, and executive implementation as related but distinct objects and lifecycles. Each must identify who may initiate it, who decides it, what authority applies, what resources it can use, when it becomes effective, and what can amend, stop, expire, or replace it.

The same policy goal may have several routes with different scope, durability, timing, political demands, and legal exposure. A regulation is not automatically effective when an executive order directs an agency to develop it. Passing authorization does not manufacture an appropriation. A court ruling can change enforceability without undoing everything that already happened.

Research the legal constraints relevant to each modeled mechanic through primary institutional sources. Record the source, applicable date, and deliberate game abstraction. Distinguish clearly between unavailable authority, genuinely disputed authority, and unlawful defiance the game intentionally permits with institutional consequences. Political capital cannot make a plainly nonexistent power lawful.

## 8. Model people, influence, and political capital coherently

Create a shared account of identity, roles, relationships, behavior, and evidence that each institution can use according to its own rules. Reconcile biography-derived ties, cabinet alignment, factions, lobbying, foreign rapport, and persistent nation relationships. Share appropriate concepts without forcing every actor through the same scoring formula.

Separate these meanings:

- Formal authority: what the office can legally decide, block, schedule, fund, or implement.
- Willingness: whether the person wants to help on this issue in the current situation.
- Trust and credibility: whose promises they believe, and why.
- Influence: whose behavior they can actually affect, in which institutions or communities.
- Political capital: the usable room to secure cooperation, supported by mandate, credibility, attention, promises, and relationships.
- Resources and capacity: what can actually be executed even when people agree.

Determine what should be stored, derived, or assessed. Avoid counting the same support once as approval, again as standing, and again as newly invented capital. If a spendable capital resource is justified, define its scope, units, earning, spending, recovery, saturation, and consequences; show why it is needed beyond existing rules. Trust, money, votes, and legal authority are not freely interchangeable.

Make influence issue-specific and uneven. An influential committee chair, party leader, respected specialist, regional governor, donor or interest-group figure, cabinet secretary, and charismatic junior legislator have different forms of reach. Support multi-step persuasion where justified, but cap propagation, avoid cycles and duplicate counting, and preserve the other actors' own interests. Losing an office can reduce formal authority while leaving personal credibility and allies intact.

Relationships should support appropriate direction, type, strength, history, context, visibility, and change. Relevant types include professional ties, mentorship, rivalry, loyalty, favors, commitments, institutional dependence, party/caucus ties, and foreign agreements. Only add relationship types with actual behaviors and a player use. Explain a consequential tie through its history rather than generating a label with no cause.

Keep the displayed neighborhood cap separate from actual behavioral reach. The current six-tie person-network limit must not make a broadly connected leader mechanically equivalent to an isolated actor. Death, office loss, replacement, and faction changes must update eligibility and reach without erasing historically valid relationships.

Traits should interact with incentives, opportunity, competence, risk, and institutions. Ambition can produce initiative, succession seeking, coalition building, rivalry, or sabotage depending on circumstances. Loyalty does not imply competence. Integrity may produce principled refusal. Corruption requires an opportunity and interacts with expected benefit, oversight, exposure, and sanctions; it must not make every corrupt person perform the same act on a timer.

An ambitious actor who turns against the administration may withhold cooperation, organize opposition, leak, resign publicly, obstruct implementation, or pursue their own agenda. Their office, access, allies, credibility, and willingness to take risks should constrain what they can do and how far it travels. A generic national penalty proportional only to ambition is insufficient. Model detection, attribution, responses, and recoverability as applicable.

Trace beneficial behavior with equal care: competent implementation, honest advice, credible brokerage, coalition maintenance, and initiative. Show why investing in a well-placed person can be effective, and why that relationship can create dependency or backlash. Compare actual results from working through a key figure versus approaching several less influential actors under equivalent constraints.

## 9. Make trust, betrayal, and reputation persist across systems

Use a confirmed deliberate attack on a previously trusting partner as an integration case. The act's violation of trust must matter independently of civilian deaths. Model the affected direction of trust, perceived hostility/threat, commitments breached, relationship history, and subsequent cooperation. Audit every consumer so diplomacy, treaties, basing, coalition support, intelligence access, trade, and NPC choices do not contradict one another.

The magnitude and response should depend on prior trust, attribution, intent, surprise, damage, agreements, justification, earlier conduct, personalities, domestic politics, dependence, and the target's options. Separate trust from fear and compliance: an intimidated government may comply while trusting less. Peace, compensation, consistent conduct, and leadership changes may affect repair without instantly erasing the record.

Distinguish confirmed attack, failed attempt, accident, disputed attribution, authorized joint action, covert activity, self-defense claims, and action against a shared enemy. Third parties must react through the relevant relationship and actual agreement. Defense treaties, economic unions, political forums, trade dependence, and coincidental membership are different reasons to react. Verify the specific shared compact and its obligations. Bound cascades and repeated penalties. Some governments may condemn, hedge, withdraw cooperation, demand assurances, or exploit the opening; universal identical reactions would lose the strategic differences.

Retain cause and timing in grievances. Audit permanent flags, history-tag classifiers, repeat-action discounts, and trust/disposition combinations so the same act cannot be ignored by one subsystem, counted twice by another, or recover merely because a display changed.

## 11. Account for real effects and distributional consequences

Trace each intervention from legal approval and funding through implementation capacity, delay, material outcomes, public perception, actor reactions, and later decisions. Separate immediate effects, recurring flows, one-time stocks, delayed effects, contingent effects, and irreversible consequences. Define the authoritative writer and units for each.

Audit the financial-band contract in packages/content/src/impact-model.ts: resolveImpact currently emits choiceBorrowing for a nonzero financial scale. Check the authored meaning against that assumption. Output lost, private property damaged, federal spending authorized, funds actually spent, and debt incurred are different quantities; one must not silently stand in for another. Preserve the documented distinction between presidential budget authority and the national books unless an explicitly justified model change replaces it.

Route material impacts by relevant exposure: region, industry, income, employment, age, household circumstances, public-service dependence, and other supported demographic attributes. Populations are overlapping groups with varied views. Do not equate demographic identity with personality, corruption, or an automatic political preference. Geography and exposure should explain why outcomes differ.

Separate receiving a benefit or harm from noticing it, attributing it to the administration, and changing political support. Good policy need not produce an instant uniform approval gain; a popular promise need not mean implementation succeeded.

Audit overlap among impact events, cohort effects, domestic signatures, gazetteer effects, sector effects, macro changes, and opinion calculations. The same loss or benefit must not be independently charged through several paths. Use source/contribution identity where needed, and preserve aggregate consistency. Explain aggregate changes and distributional differences from the actual applied record.

Correct reversal semantics. Revocation, expiration, judicial relief, and repeal should remove appropriate continuing authority/effects; they cannot automatically restore lives, refund spent money, cancel borrowed debt, or erase unrelated later interventions. Test saturation/clamping, partial implementation, multiple overlapping policies, and repeated cancellation. Descriptions must match what the engine actually retained or reversed.

Represent a remedy's scope in the effect contract: affected provision, application, jurisdiction, party, implementation phase, and effective date as appropriate. Limited relief must target applicable contributions rather than reverse the whole order or statute. Untouched provisions continue. Authorization must also be idempotent: a double click, retried commit, stale preview, or save/reload retry cannot charge or apply an act twice. Distinguish a later intentional reissuance from retrying the same action.
