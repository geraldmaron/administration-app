## What changed, by item

**R1 (commission removal).** The FTC changed on the holding. SEC, FCC, CFPB, CFTC, FDIC and NRC changed on the same reasoning (each exercises rulemaking, enforcement and civil litigation) plus *Seila Law* for the single-director CFPB; no opinion names them, and the lane doc says so. **Left unchanged and recorded:** the Federal Reserve (Governors for cause, Chair term-protected; reserved in *Slaughter*, protected on the interim record in *Cook*) and the NLRB (adjudicatory; its status was left undecided and the Court has only let removals stand on the interim docket in *Wilcox*). Chair designation and membership move together: the catalog models one chair office per commission and `removeOfficer` reads it. Structural `independence` is unchanged for every commission (insulated delivery, no docket OMB review), so the change is confined to the removal rule and direction. Removing an at-will commission chair writes `INDEPENDENT_AT_WILL_POLITICIZATION` (0.1), below the cheapest for-cause removal.

**R2 (commission direction).** `isDirectionBarred(def)` is true only for an independent body whose head is protected from removal. Policy supervision (`enforcement-posture`, `sanctions-tempo`) at SEC, FTC, FCC, CFPB, CFTC, FDIC and NRC is carried or slow-walked like any executive body's. Named-matter instructions are not offered to commissions (their matters are decided by a vote or an adjudication), so there is no separate adjudication path to model.

**R3 (Justice).** The DOJ entry is gone from `STATUTORY_BARS`. `investigation` and `targeting` at DOJ and its components go through the Attorney General's (or acting Attorney General's) judgment, which reuses the `certify-order` weighing with `reach` 0.4 (investigation) or 0.7 (targeting), `accumulated` 0.2 per earlier casework instruction at the same body, `ground` "silent". A principled or low-deference Attorney General declines (the directive is `refused`, signed by "The Attorney General", with the existing refusal costs and the second-refusal pattern matter); a deferential one carries it, which bends the department (politicization and oversight pressure by the kind's weight) and opens the pattern matter on the second carried instruction. The IRS bar (26 U.S.C. 7217) and the Fed bar are untouched. Nothing in the path yields a charge, plea or conviction; the narration and refusal text say a grand jury and the courts decide. Not done: Attorney General resignation as a third outcome; career-resignation exposure.

**R4 (inspector general).** `fileInspectorRemovalNotice` takes an `InspectorRemovalAccount` (a ground and particulars, each citing an oversight matter and a week). A bare sentence, or an account with no particular, is refused ("state your reasons"). A filed account is judged only on records the campaign holds (`accountDefects`): conclusory ground, a cited record that does not exist, conduct dated outside the inspector's tenure, or a cited record that is the inspector's own audit. A deficient notice is filed, recorded as deficient on the `InspectorGeneral` (`noticeDeficiencies`) and costs the Hill 0.06 more. No regex and no length rule. **Limit:** the engine holds no record of an inspector general's own conduct, so today every account is deficient unless it cites a non-audit matter at that office; sufficiency is reachable once such records exist. Court exposure is not modeled (the proceeding subject kinds are tied to orders and bills).

**X2 (special counsel).** New `superviseSpecialCounsel(campaign, {kind: "countermand" | "remove", ...})`. The Attorney General decides through the same weighing (reach 0.5 or 0.7, plus 0.25 when the inquiry is aimed at the administration); a declined request is recorded on the matter and costs nothing; a carried removal closes the matter, marks it public and (when aimed at the administration) obstructed, and costs the Hill 0.12 (+0.10 aimed at the administration); a countermand leaves the counsel sitting and costs 0.06. The President has no direct verb.

**X3 (acting appointments).** `actingClassOf` admits only the first assistant (the bench's first name), a person confirmed to another Senate-confirmed office, or a senior bench employee; anyone else is refused. A nominee for the office may not act unless the first assistant. `actingLapseDay` counts the 210 days from the day the vacancy opened and applies 3349a for a vacancy in the first 60 days. **Limits:** extension of the clock while a first or second nomination is pending (3346(a)(2), (b)) belongs to the nomination life cycle and is not applied; first-assistant tenure (90 days) is assumed for the bench's first name.

**C1 (impoundment).** `proposeRescission` and `proposeDeferral` in `appropriation.ts` (the existing path); `impoundment.ts` runs the weekly clock inside `budgetPhase`; `congressional-session.ts` counts days of continuous session. A held message lowers `selectSpendableBudget`, which the outlook and the weekly spend now read. The 45-day window, the one count at day 25, default release, the "no second rescission" rule, pocket rescission (flagged contested, lapses at fiscal year end), deferral purposes, the Comptroller General's report (day 14 of session) and suit (25 session days later) and a court order (56 calendar days later) are modeled. The Antideficiency Act is not engaged by withholding and is not modeled. **Engine finding:** the engine had no congressional recess schedule (the signing window file says "the game does not adjourn Congress"), so `congressional-session.ts` is the first. Its windows are the standard session calendar's adjournments of more than three days; pro forma sittings (which in current practice keep most recesses from breaking the count, and under which the 2025 package ran about 45 calendar days) are not modeled. Whether to model them is the one calibration decision for the owner.

## Wave 3

**BL-307 (acting clock and nominations).** `appointments.ts#actingClockEffects`, called from `nomination.ts` (the weekly phase and `withdrawNomination`), applies 5 U.S.C. 3346(a)(2) and (b) as opened in Wave 2: submission (entering the `nominated` stage) of a first or second nomination for the office since the vacancy tolls the acting officer's `actingExpiresDay` (cleared while pending); rejection, withdrawal or return of a submitted first or second nomination restarts 210 days from that day; a third nomination, or a name withdrawn before submission, changes nothing. First-assistant tenure is still assumed for the bench's first name. Amendment for BL-307 acceptance: closed by this commit with the tests "tolls the 210 days ..." and "restarts nothing ..." in `appointments.test.ts`.

**2R leftovers.** When a nominee stops acting (`nominationEndsActing`), the next eligible name on the bench (3345(a), excluding the nominee and pending nominees) is seated the same day; only when none exists does the office wait, and the feed says its statutory functions fall to the head of the agency alone (3348(b)(2)). A repeated policy deferral of the same account in a fiscal year, after the Comptroller General's unlawful finding, now costs the Hill 0.02 per earlier finding, capped at the first finding's 0.08 (`REPEAT_UNLAWFUL_DEFERRAL_STEP`; a design judgment).
