# W4 retime review record (BL-251, commit-time copy)

Reviewed October 17, 2026, from a session that did not draft or revise. Scope: the 43 scenarios in job `w4-retime`, 64 changed fields (`scenarios retime-report w4-retime`). Standard: `docs/CONTENT_DOCTRINE.md` §10.1.

## Method

1. Compared every `content/jobs/work/w4-retime/*.out.json` with the same id in `content/corpus/shards/*.jsonl`, key by key. All 43 differ only in `context.inaction`, `replies[].commitment.rejoinder.text` and `replies[].commitment.remembered`. No `press`, price, cast, gate or flag changed. Check (1) holds for all 43.
2. Read all 64 before/after pairs, and for the contested ones read the scenario's opening, standing paragraph and the reply it sets, to see whether a "new" fact is already in the week.
3. Question applied to each: is the new text true tonight, with any forecast hedged and in a person's mouth; does it add a fact a later scenario must honor; does it still read as the same scenario.

## Pattern note

"Already" appears in 16 of the 64 new texts and in 1 of the 64 originals. It is the retimer's main device: a later outcome becomes a present-perfect fact. That is fine when the reaction can happen within hours of the order (a letter, a statement, a crew on site). It is a disguised flash-forward when the reaction needs days or a count of people to accrue. The nine rejections below are all of the second kind or invent a fact. A lighter second device, "if X, then Y" or "expects", is used well in `the-generators-that-failed`, `the-seat-that-outlived-the-judge-who-filled-it`, `the-officer-offering-to-walk-across`, `the-harvest-fails` and `the-last-building-on-the-block`.
