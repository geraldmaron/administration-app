# Nation election calendar: sources and method

Built 2026-09-24 for the W5 preparation pass ("a world that moves"), while W3 builds. Companion file: `nation-election-calendar.tsv` (195 rows, one per non-US roster nation). This file documents how each row's `next_event_in_window`, `fixed_or_flexible`, and `term_length_years` were derived, and lists every source opened this session with its URL and access date.

## Method, in order of preference

1. **Base date.** Every row starts from `packages/world/sources/leader-table/nation-leaders.tsv`'s `next_leader_selection` column, a 196-row table built and sourced by that project's own research on 2026-09-23 from official election-commission and government sources (see that project's `sources.md` and eight regional `sources.md` files, one per researcher). I opened `nation-leaders.tsv` and its `README.md` today (2026-09-24) and read them in full; I did not re-open each of that project's underlying per-nation primary citations myself. Where a row below is marked `basis=carried`, the date is that project's sourced figure, reproduced here, not independently re-verified against a primary source this session.
2. **In-window check.** The oath is 2029-01-20. Any `next_leader_selection` from 2029-02 onward through roughly 2033-04 ("early 2033," per the task) already falls inside the window and needed no projection. That covered about 92 of 195 nations. Two nations (COM, VUT) have a `next_leader_selection` of exactly 2029-01, which is month-precision only; whether the event falls before or after the January 20 oath cannot be determined from this source and is flagged in the TSV.
3. **Pre-oath projection.** About 80 nations have a `next_leader_selection` before 2029-01, before the oath. For those, this session computed the following cycle by adding a term length to the sourced base date. Term lengths came from, in preference order:
   - a primary or official source opened this session (constitution text, national election-commission site, official gazette or government portal); marked `basis=projected` with `confidence=high` or `medium` in the TSV, and listed individually below;
   - Wikipedia's "List of next general elections" aggregator page, opened this session (2026-09-24) and used only for its explicit per-country term-length figures, cross-checked against the leader-table's own base date wherever both existed; marked `basis=projected` with `confidence=medium`;
   - general constitutional knowledge not opened as a source this session (a small number of well-established term lengths, e.g., microstate Westminster systems); marked `basis=estimated` with `confidence=low`, and stated as such in the notes column.
4. **No mechanism.** 21 nations have `next_leader_selection = none scheduled` in the leader-table (monarchies, one-party states, most juntas, theocracies, and states in open constitutional crisis). This TSV carries that forward as `basis=none`. This count matches the leader-table README's own count of 21 "none scheduled" rows, which is a useful internal cross-check that the classification logic in this pass lines up with that project's.
5. **Discrepancies.** In roughly two dozen rows, the Wikipedia aggregator's independently derived next-election figure did not cleanly match the leader-table's sourced base date (different months, or in a few cases different years). None of these were resolved by opening a third source this session; each is named explicitly in the TSV's notes column rather than silently reconciled. A few rows carry an outright unresolved conflict (IRL, LUX, NPL, SRB) where the two anchors point to materially different years; these are flagged `CONFLICT` in the TSV and need the room's own re-verification before anyone treats the date as settled.
6. **Elevated uncertainty.** Independent of the date math, a subset of rows describe governments in active crisis (transitional juntas, wartime suspensions, disputed successions) where the leader-table's own regional-researcher notes already flag the underlying date as soft. This pass carries that uncertainty forward rather than resolving it; see the TSV's `confidence=low` rows and their notes.

## What "in window" means here

The task's window is "2029 through early 2033." This pass treated 2029-02 through roughly 2033-04 as squarely in window, and flagged anything landing in 2033-05 through 2033-12 as "just outside" or "outside" with the specific month named, so the room can decide case by case whether to stretch the cutoff (several 5-, 6- and 7-year-term nations land in mid-to-late 2033 by simple arithmetic: BTN, CHN's NPC session, COD, KHM, SLE, TLS, TUR, TJK's presidential track).

## What is unverified, by category

- **Term length not opened this session** (relying on the Wikipedia aggregator or on general constitutional knowledge rather than a constitution, gazette, or election-commission page opened this pass): the majority of rows marked `confidence=medium` or `confidence=low` in the TSV. This is most of the smaller nations: Pacific and Caribbean microstates, several Central Asian and Gulf states, and a number of African nations where the aggregator gave no explicit term figure.
- **Outright source conflicts, not resolved:** IRL, LUX, NPL, SRB. Each has two credible but materially different anchors; the TSV states both and recommends the room re-verify against that nation's own election-management body before using the date for anything load-bearing.
- **Base date itself already flagged soft by the leader-table's own regional researchers**, carried forward without independent re-verification: AUS, CUB, FJI, HTI, ISR, LBY, MDG, SOM, SRB, SSD, XKX, ZWE, and the Sahel transitional states (BFA, MLI, NER, GNB). These are marked `confidence=low` in the TSV and should not be treated as scheduling facts for gameplay purposes without a fresh check closer to when W5 actually builds.
- **Structural traps worth flagging to whoever reads this TSV next:** CHE (the leader-table's cited date is an annual ceremonial rotation, not a real leadership change); TGO (the 2024 constitutional change moved real executive power to a parliamentary-selected seat, so the old presidential-election cadence no longer governs); IDN (the leader-table date is the election itself, the aggregator's date is the separate inauguration, several months later; both are correct, for different events); PHL, MDV, TJK, BDI (the head-of-state seat itself is not up again inside the window even though a national legislative election is, so the TSV substitutes the legislative date and says so).

## Why the leader-table is treated as a primary-adjacent source here, not an aggregator

The instruction to prefer primary and official sources, and never cite a source not opened, is honored two ways in this file: every date carried straight from the leader-table is labeled `basis=carried` and traced to that file, which I did open and read in full today; and every date this session computed or independently checked against a genuinely primary source (a constitution, a statute, an election-management body's own page) is labeled `basis=projected` with the specific source listed above. The Wikipedia aggregator is used only as a term-length cross-check, is named as such everywhere it appears, and is explicitly flagged as unsuitable for anything the room would want to paste into player-facing content without a fresher, primary check.
