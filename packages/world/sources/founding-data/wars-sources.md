# Sources for war-durations.tsv and carried-conflicts.tsv

Built 2026-09-24, read-only research for W5 prep while W3 builds. Every source below was either **opened directly** this session (WebFetch on the page, or WebFetch-then-Read on a downloaded PDF, in which case I read the actual extracted text/pages) or **search-summarized only** (a WebSearch result gave me an aggregated answer without my opening the underlying page). The two are marked explicitly on every row; never assume "found by search" means "opened." No real person's name appears in this file. The revision pass (2026-09-24) removed the article authors' names and withheld the two URLs whose host or file name carries a name; each source is cited by title, journal and year instead. A second pass, also 2026-09-24 (the historian's verification session; see `verification-2026-09-24.md`), re-opened the two fast-moving war rows directly and adds sources C22-C26 below.

## Duration sources (war-durations.tsv, prefix D)

**D1: freerangestats.info, "Durations of wars" (posted under a 2026-05-13 dateline; the underlying data is Correlates of War inter-state war data, 1823-2003).**
URL: https://freerangestats.info/blog/2026/05/13/war-durations; opened 2026-09-24 (WebFetch, opened directly).
Carries: median 139 days, mean 408 days, min 4 days (1969 "Football War"), max 3734 days (Vietnam War Phase II), computed by the post's author directly from COW inter-state war data. No quartile (p25/p75) breakdown was given by this source; flagged as a gap in war-durations.tsv rather than invented.

**D2: The Correlates of War Project, "COW War Data, 1816-2007 (v4.0, Intra-State v5.1)."**
URL: https://correlatesofwar.org/data-sets/cow-war/; opened 2026-09-24 (WebFetch, opened directly).
Carries: the dataset family overview (inter-state, intra-state, extra-state, non-state war data, version numbers and coverage years) used to cross-check D1's and D4's sample framing. No duration summary statistics are on this page itself.

**D3: "Military Intervention by Powerful States, 1945-2003" (2009), Journal of Peace Research 46(5): 707-718 (the MIPS dataset).**
URL: an open PDF mirror on a University of North Carolina faculty site (withheld in the revision pass: its host and file name carry the author's name, as C1's does; the journal citation locates it); opened 2026-09-24 (WebFetch downloaded the PDF, then Read extracted and I read all 13 pages, including Table II's duration-by-objective breakdown).
Carries: 126 major-power (US/UK/France/Russia-USSR/China) military interventions, 1945-2003. Table II gives median intervention duration in days by primary political objective (PPO): maintain foreign regime 309, remove foreign regime 184, policy change 195, acquire/defend territory 118, maintain empire 890, social protection/order 296, all 211. Prose gives "on average... just over two years" for the mean, "less than seven months" for the median, shortest interventions "only a few days," longest "more than a decade." This is the strongest primary-ish source I could open for the "intervention" war type; an academic dataset paper with a duration table I read myself, not a search summary.

**D4: "Why Do Some Civil Wars Last So Much Longer Than Others?" (2004), Journal of Peace Research 41(3): 275-301.**
URL: an open PDF mirror on a personal academic site (withheld in the revision pass: its host and file name are people's names; the journal citation locates it); opened 2026-09-24 (WebFetch downloaded the PDF, then Read extracted and I read all 8 pages of the article body, including Table I).
Carries: 128 civil wars, 1945-1999 (the civil war list the article's author compiled with a coauthor, >=1,000 killed over the war's course, >=100 average annual, >=100 on both sides). Raw/uncorrected median 5.5 years, mean 8.8 years (biased downward by 25 cases still ongoing/right-censored when coded). Weibull survival-model estimate correcting for censoring: median 7.1 years, mean 11.1 years; this is the article's own preferred "typical" figure. Coup/popular-revolution wars: median 2.1 years, mean 3 years (n=22: 19 coup-related, 3 popular-revolution). Non-coup/non-revolution wars: median 9.0 years, mean 12.9 years. Anti-colonial wars: median 4.7 years, mean 7.3 years (n=13). Table I gives a full regional breakdown (Weibull-based median/mean/N) for Eastern Europe, North Africa/Middle East, a mixed Western-bloc region, Latin America, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Asia; reproduced in war-durations.tsv. This is the single strongest source in the whole file: I read the full article text and the actual table, not a summary of it.

**D5: (2002), "Third-party Interventions and the Duration of Intrastate Conflicts," Journal of Conflict Resolution 46(1): 55-73.**
NOT independently opened this session. Every WebFetch attempt (a scispace PDF mirror, a Gettysburg-hosted student paper citing it) either returned empty content or a 403. What appears in war-durations.tsv is a WebSearch-engine aggregated summary: 150 conflicts, 1944-1999, 101 with an outside intervention, hazard-model finding that interventions tend to lengthen rather than shorten expected duration. Treated as direction-only, low confidence, no weeks-level number used from it.

**D6: (2000), "Third-Party Intervention and the Civil War Process," International Studies Quarterly.**
NOT independently opened this session (no successful fetch was attempted that returned readable content). What appears in war-durations.tsv is a WebSearch-engine aggregated summary: 152 civil wars, 1820-1992, finding that government-only third-party support lengthens wars and balanced interventions have a different effect. Treated as direction-only, low confidence, alongside D5.

**D7: This program's own measurement: `waves/w2/2B/sec7-duration-report.json`.**
Path: `<local path>`; opened directly (Read tool) 2026-09-24.
Carries: the game engine's own measured war durations at HEAD-then, five-seed and supplementary twenty-five-seed runs over 104 turns. Median 6.5-7 weeks, p90 8, all measured wars (4 of 4, then 13 of 13) ending in defender victory. This is the baseline D14 and W5/5C are calibrating against, already cited in the register (`README.md:858`, `:1503`) and the plan audit (`audit-plan.md:155`).

**D8: Correlates of War Project, "Extra-state Wars (Version 4.0): Definitions and Variables," codebook.**
URL: https://correlatesofwar.org/wp-content/uploads/Extra-StateWars_Codebook.pdf; opened 2026-09-24 (WebFetch downloaded, Read extracted all 9 pages).
Carries: the COW definition of an extra-state war (a state fighting a non-state entity outside its borders; colonial vs. imperial subtypes) and confirms 163 extra-state wars in COW v4.0 (1816-2007). No duration summary statistics are in this codebook; it is a definitions/variable-layout document, used here only to confirm what "extra-state war" does and does not cover, and to rule out an inaccurate "26 months average" figure a search engine had attributed to a separate, unopened thesis (see the note at the end of this file).

**D9: (2012), "How persistent is armed conflict?" ERSA Working Paper 311.**
URL (open mirror): https://econrsa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/wp311.pdf; opened 2026-09-24 (WebFetch downloaded, Read extracted the first 6 pages).
Carries: confirms COW's four war types (inter-, intra-, extra-, non-state) and a total of 653 unique COW wars of all types, 1816-2007, coded across 789 present-day-country locales. This paper's "median 3, mean 7" figure is total pre-1945 war-YEARS experienced by a LOCALE (a country's cumulative history of hosting fighting), not a single war's duration; it is NOT used as a war-duration statistic anywhere in war-durations.tsv, specifically because a WebSearch summary of a different, unopened source (the OpenBU thesis) initially conflated the two and I caught the discrepancy only by opening this paper myself and reading the actual sentence. This is the clearest example in this research pass of why the "never cite what you didn't open" rule matters.

**Explicitly dropped:** (1996), "The Duration of Interstate Wars, 1816-1985," American Political Science Review 90(2): 239-257. Searched but never opened (paywalled at Cambridge Core, JSTOR, ProQuest); no number from it appears anywhere in this file. D1 supplied a usable, openly-read alternative for the interstate figure instead.

**Explicitly unverified and excluded:** an OpenBU master's thesis, "The neglected war: intervention and extra-state war duration, 1816-2007" (Boston University, 2017), which a WebSearch summary attributed an "average extra-state war duration of about 26 months" to. Every attempt to open it directly (the bitstream URL, the item URL, the handle URL) returned HTTP 403. That figure does **not** appear in war-durations.tsv. If a future pass can actually open this thesis, it would be a useful fifth "intervention/colonial-war" comparison point.

**Also searched but not used:** RAND MG965 ("How Insurgencies End" / "Paths to Victory," 2010); every URL 403'd; a WebSearch summary reported a median insurgency length of about ten years across 89 cases. Not used because I could not open and verify it myself.

## Reading notes for whoever builds `seed/opening-world.ts` from this

- **The register already has an opinion on Israel** (D3: "Israel modeled as `declared:false`"). Nothing in carried-conflicts.tsv should be read as arguing against that; several rows above involve Israel as a combatant in the real world, and the existing design choice presumably exists precisely because Israel's involvement is dense enough across multiple fronts (Gaza, Lebanon, Iran) that a single `declared:true` flag would misrepresent it either way.
- **This file's "as of" dates are already stale the day it ships.** Every war row above is mid-negotiation, mid-ceasefire, or mid-collapse as of 2026-09-24; none is a settled fact the way "World War II ended in 1945" is settled. Re-check the fast-moving rows (Iran, Russia-Ukraine, Gaza) close to whenever `seed/opening-world.ts` is actually written, rather than trusting this file's dates at face value that far out. **This held true even within the same calendar day**: the second pass, run hours after the first, found the Iran war had moved from "day 207" to a formal written ceasefire roadmap with a four-to-five-day clock on it, and found Russia's government had rejected the one concrete ceasefire proposal on the table. Whoever builds the seed should re-check again, not trust either pass's snapshot.
- **Confidence labels are about sourcing discipline, not event importance.** A "medium" confidence row (built from a WebSearch summary rather than an opened page) can still be the right item to seed; it just means someone should open the underlying page directly before a specific number or date from that row becomes a hard-coded value anywhere in the engine or content.
- **The crude-price gap.** The Iran war's day-209 update puts Brent-adjacent crude at $103.08 on September 23 2026 — well above the $91 August 2026 figure `price-anchors.tsv` carries from the EIA's September 9 Short-Term Energy Outlook. That STEO figure was accurate when read; it is a forecast series overtaken by an actual price move tied to this file's own war row. Whoever prices D11/D14's Gulf-shock fixture should treat $91 as the anchor's vintage, not the current price, and should not need to reopen this file to know that: it's flagged here and in `verification-2026-09-24.md`.
