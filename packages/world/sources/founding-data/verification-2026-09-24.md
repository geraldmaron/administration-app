## 2. The Iran war, as sourced (day 209, 2026-09-24)

This is the state D3 needs (`../scope.md` Q2). It supersedes the first
pass's search-only C5/C6.

- **No ceasefire is in effect.** The war has run since 2026-02-28 (day 209
  on September 24). At least three prior ceasefire attempts (April, June,
  and an implied further one) each collapsed.
- **Iran formally proposed a written roadmap the same day (2026-09-24):**
  a regionwide ceasefire of up to sixty days, a phased reopening of the
  Strait of Hormuz, an end to the blockade, unfreezing of frozen assets,
  and an agreed timeline for substantive negotiations — conveyed after
  direct US-Iran contact at the UN General Assembly (the first
  acknowledged contact since June), mediated by Pakistan, Qatar and Egypt.
- **A contradiction sits inside Iran's own offer.** Its Supreme National
  Security Council separately attached a four-to-five-day deadline for US
  acceptance, which reads as inconsistent with a proposal whose own terms
  allow negotiation *during* a longer ceasefire. Domestic division in
  Tehran over whether the talks were even authorized was reported the same
  day.
- **Iran's claimed Strait of Hormuz reopening does not match shipping
  data.** The government claims 60-70% of pre-war flow restored; independent
  transit data shows one commercial transit on September 20 against an
  approximately 85-a-day pre-war baseline, with about 396 vessels idle
  under a "SEVERE" maritime advisory.
- **An informal lull, not a ceasefire, has held for roughly two-and-a-half
  weeks.** No strikes, ballistic launches or drone attacks against the
  reading government's forces were reported for fifteen to nineteen
  consecutive days heading into day 209.
- **US posture:** the CENTCOM Middle East buildup (multiple carrier strike
  groups, reinforced air-defense batteries, fighters based in Israel for
  the first time in this dataset's coverage) is still described as active
  and elevated, with no stated drawdown as of this session.
- **Price effect, flagged for 5E/D11/D14:** crude oil rose 3.9% to $103.08
  on September 23 2026 — well above the $91 August 2026 figure
  `price-anchors.tsv` carries from the EIA's September 9 Short-Term Energy
  Outlook. That $91 figure was accurate when read; it is a forecast series
  a real price move has since overtaken. Anyone pricing D11/D14's Gulf
  shock after this date should treat $91 as the anchor's *vintage*, not
  today's price. This is outside this file's edit scope (`price-anchors.tsv`
  is not in the files I was asked to touch) but is load-bearing enough to
  flag explicitly.

**Reading for D3.** The recommended option in `../scope.md` Q2 — the Iran
war carried as "a held front at low intensity, with the United States not a
belligerent at the oath" — is not contradicted by what this session found,
but it is also not confirmed as a settled fact: the war is still legally
"active" (day 209, no ceasefire) with a live, contradictory diplomatic
process on the table. A campaign opening in January 2029 is about two years
past this file's window, so nothing here settles whether the war is over,
frozen, or restarted by then; it only confirms that "active but no fresh
strikes for weeks, with a fragile and self-contradicting diplomatic track"
is the honestly-sourced September 2026 starting point.

## 3. Russia-Ukraine, as sourced (2026-09-23/24)

- **No ceasefire is in effect.** A roughly 750-mile front is described by
  two independently opened trackers (CFR, ISW/Kyiv Post) as a largely
  frozen stalemate, with a February 2026 Ukrainian gain in Zaporizhzhia
  (about 100 square miles) the one exception either source names.
- **The one concrete proposal on the table was rejected within days.** On
  September 23 2026, senior Kremlin officials "effectively rejected" a
  US-Ukrainian-proposed limited "energy" ceasefire (no strikes on power,
  heating or water infrastructure); Russia's foreign minister stated
  operations would not pause during talks.
- **A NATO-adjacent escalation the same week:** a Russian helicopter's
  incursion into Polish airspace near Kaliningrad, read by ISW as a further
  escalation toward a NATO member.
- **Talks are floated, not scheduled.** Both governments have signaled
  openness to resuming contact, possibly in October 2026, but no date or
  level of seniority is confirmed as of this session.

This corrects the first pass's `RUS-UKR` dyad row, which cited only `GEN`
(no dated 2026 source) despite the pair's obvious salience; it now cites
two directly opened, dated trackers.

## 5. Corrections found and made

1. **`USA-SAU`'s treaty date was wrong.** The row said "Strategic Defense
   Agreement (May 2025)." The official White House fact sheet is dated
   November 18 2025 and states the agreement was signed that day, alongside
   a major non-NATO ally designation. May 2025 was a separate, earlier
   arms-purchase and investment announcement from an earlier Riyadh visit,
   not this agreement. **Corrected in `dyads.tsv`**; the general
   characterization ("historic," "stops short of a mutual-defense
   guarantee") held up and was not itself wrong, only the date.
2. **`RUS-UKR` had no dated 2026 source at all**, despite being one of the
   two live interstate wars the whole carried-conflicts table exists to
   capture. Now cites two directly opened trackers (§3 above).
3. **`ISR-SYR` had no dated 2026 source.** Now cites the two governments'
   own September 2026 UN statements (Golan buffer-zone dispute), opened
   directly.
4. **`VEN-GUY`'s hearing status was stale by one step.** The row said
   hearings "opened May 2026"; the ICJ's own case page shows hearings
   *concluded* May 11 2026 and a judgment is now pending (the claimant's
   counsel has publicly cited an expectation of judgment by or before
   January 2027). Corrected in `dyads.tsv`.
5. **All fifteen sanctions-program dates in `carried-conflicts.tsv` were
   re-checked against the live OFAC page and matched exactly.** Zero
   corrections needed there — this is a confirmation, not a correction,
   but it's worth recording that the prior pass's primary-source table held
   up under a second, independent re-open.
6. **No other row's *content* was found to be false** by anything opened
   this session (as opposed to merely thin/unconfirmed). Several rows
   (Pakistan-Afghanistan, Armenia-Azerbaijan, Serbia-Kosovo, Israel-Lebanon)
   gained a materially fuller, fresher picture without the earlier
   description being wrong — see the individual `dyads.tsv` row edits.

`../scope.md` Q2's text (the "state at the W3 close" placeholder) and into
whatever fills the "Probe result" line in the Tail-shape question — neither

For `scenario-writer` and `analyst`: the load-bearing details from this
session are (1) the Iran ceasefire roadmap's internal contradiction (a
short deadline bolted onto a longer framework) is a *shape* — a real
diplomatic offer that undercuts its own credibility — that can be recast
onto any two governments in a live-conflict scenario without naming Iran or
the United States; (2) the Hormuz-reopening claim versus shipping-data
contradiction is a reusable "claimed de-escalation the numbers don't
support" shape; (3) the Saudi-Pakistan pact's own sourcing problem (a
mutual-defense pledge whose operative text has never been published) is a
reusable "ally commitment nobody can actually read" shape for a alliance-
decision scenario.
